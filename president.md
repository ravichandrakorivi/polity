# President of India

## 1. Constitutional Position

- The President is the **Head of the Indian State** and the constitutional head of the Union executive.
- **Article 52:** There shall be a President of India.
- **Article 53:** The executive power of the Union is vested in the President.
- These powers are generally exercised on the **aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister**.
- The President is a constituent part of Parliament but is **not a member of either House**.

> **President:** Head of State.  
> **Prime Minister:** Head of Government.

### Relationship with the Council of Ministers

- **Article 74:** Ministerial advice is binding on the President.
- The President may require the Council of Ministers to **reconsider its advice once**.
- Advice tendered after reconsideration is binding.
- Courts cannot inquire into what ministerial advice was tendered. However, governmental action is not thereby exempt from judicial review.

| Amendment | Significance |
|---|---|
| 42nd Amendment, 1976 | Expressly made ministerial advice binding |
| 44th Amendment, 1978 | Expressly permitted the President to require reconsideration |

---

## 2. Election of the President

- The **Election Commission of India** conducts the election.

### Electoral College — Article 54

The President is elected by:

1. Elected members of the **Lok Sabha**.
2. Elected members of the **Rajya Sabha**.
3. Elected members of **State Legislative Assemblies**.
4. Elected members of the Legislative Assemblies of **Delhi and Puducherry**.

### Who Does Not Participate?

- Nominated members of either House of Parliament.
- Nominated members of Legislative Assemblies.
- Members of State Legislative Councils.
- Former MLAs of a dissolved Legislative Assembly.

> The electoral college does not automatically include every Union Territory merely because it has a Legislative Assembly.

### Election Method — Article 55

- **Proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.**
- **Secret ballot.**
- Electors indicate preferences among candidates.
- The result depends on the **value of votes**, not merely the number of electors supporting a candidate.

### Value of Votes

| Elector | Basis of vote value |
|---|---|
| Elected MLA | Relevant state population and number of elected Assembly members |
| Elected MP | Total value of votes assigned to elected MLAs divided by the total number of elected MPs |

- All elected MLAs **within the same state** have equal vote value.
- MLA vote values may differ **between states**.
- All elected MPs have equal vote value, irrespective of their House or state.

The basic calculation for an MLA is:

$$
\text{Value of an MLA's vote}
=
\frac{\text{Relevant population of the state}}
{\text{Number of elected MLAs}\times 1000}
$$

- Apply the constitutional rounding rule: a remainder of **500 or more** in the population-per-MLA calculation adds one vote.
- For an MP’s vote value, a fraction **exceeding one-half** is rounded up; other fractions are disregarded.

### Population Basis

- The **1971 Census** population remains the basis until the relevant figures of the **first census taken after 2026** are published.
- The passage of the year 2026 alone does not change the population basis.

## 3. Qualifications and Nomination

### Qualifications — Article 58

A candidate must:

- Be an **Indian citizen**.
- Have completed **35 years**.
- Be qualified for election as a member of the **Lok Sabha**.
- Not hold a disqualifying **office of profit**.

For this purpose, holding any of the following offices does not itself disqualify a candidate:

- President.
- Vice-President.
- Governor.
- Union or State Minister.

> A candidate need not already be a Member of Parliament.

### Nomination Requirements

- At least **50 electors as proposers**.
- At least **50 electors as seconders**.
- Security deposit: **₹15,000**.

### Important Distinctions

- A candidate need not already be a Member of Parliament.
- Eligibility for election to the Rajya Lok Sabha does not reduce the minimum age for President to 25.
- **President:** Must be qualified for election to the Lok Sabha.
- **Vice-President:** Must be qualified for election to the Rajya Sabha.

---

## 4. Oath and Conditions of Office

### Oath — Article 60

The oath is made before:

1. The **Chief Justice of India**; or
2. In the CJI’s absence, the **senior-most available Supreme Court judge**.

The President undertakes to:

- Faithfully execute the office.
- Preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law.
- Devote oneself to the service and well-being of the people of India.

A person acting as President or discharging presidential functions must also take the prescribed oath.

### Conditions — Article 59

- Cannot be a member of Parliament or a State Legislature.
- If such a member becomes President, the legislative seat is vacated on **entering presidential office**, not on declaration of the election result.
- Cannot hold another office of profit.
- Emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during the term.

---

## 5. Term, Resignation and Vacancy

| Matter | Provision |
|---|---|
| Normal term | Five years from entering office |
| Resignation addressed to | Vice-President |
| Re-election | Permitted; no constitutional limit on number of terms |
| Successor not yet in office | Incumbent continues until the successor enters office |
| Election for normal expiry | Must be completed before the existing term expires |
| Election for a casual vacancy | As soon as possible, and within six months |
| Term after election to a casual vacancy | Full five years from entering office |

### Acting Arrangements

- The **Vice-President acts as President** when a vacancy arises through death, resignation, removal or otherwise.
- During the President’s temporary inability, such as illness or absence, the Vice-President discharges presidential functions.
- If both offices are vacant, the **CJI**, or in the CJI’s absence the **senior-most available Supreme Court judge**, discharges the functions under the statutory arrangement.

> Expiry of the President’s five-year term does not automatically make the Vice-President acting President.

---

## 6. Election Disputes — Article 71

- Decided by the **Supreme Court**; its decision is final.
- An election cannot be challenged merely because a vacancy existed in the electoral college.
- If the election is declared void, official acts performed before the Court’s decision are not invalidated merely for that reason.

---

## 7. Impeachment — Article 61

### Ground

**Violation of the Constitution.**

The Constitution does not define this expression.

### Procedure

1. Either House of Parliament may initiate the charge.
2. The notice must be signed by at least **one-fourth of the total membership** of that House.
3. At least **14 days’ notice** is required.
4. The initiating House must pass the resolution by **at least two-thirds of its total membership**.
5. The other House investigates the charge or causes it to be investigated.
6. The President has the right to appear and be represented.
7. If the investigating House sustains the charge by **at least two-thirds of its total membership**, removal takes effect.

### Election versus Impeachment

| Category | Presidential election | Impeachment |
|---|---|---|
| Elected MPs | Participate | Participate |
| Nominated MPs | Do not participate | Participate |
| Eligible elected MLAs | Participate | Do not participate |
| Members of Legislative Councils | Do not participate | Do not participate |

> **Impeachment majority:** Two-thirds of total membership, not merely two-thirds of members present and voting.

**Note:**

- "Two-thirds of the total membership of each House” is a type of special majority. It is required for the impeachment of the President under Article 61.
Here, total membership includes vacant seats, absent members and members who abstain from voting.

| Type of majority | Votes required |
|---|---|
| **Simple majority** | More than half of members present and voting |
| **Effective majority** | More than half of total membership **excluding vacant seats** |
| **Absolute majority** | More than half of total membership |
| **Special majority under Article 61** | **At least two-thirds of total membership** |

## 8. Executive Powers

The President:

- Appoints the Prime Minister.
- Appoints other Union Ministers on the Prime Minister’s advice.
- Appoints Governors, the Attorney General, CAG, Supreme Court and High Court judges, and other specified constitutional authorities.
- Makes rules for the transaction and allocation of Union government business.
- Appoints administrators of Union Territories, subject to constitutional arrangements.
- May establish an Inter-State Council under Article 263.

All executive action of the Government of India is expressed to be taken **in the President’s name**.

### Information from the Prime Minister — Article 78

The President may require the Prime Minister to:

- Communicate decisions of the Council of Ministers.
- Furnish information about Union administration and legislative proposals.
- Submit a matter decided by an individual Minister to the Council of Ministers for consideration.

> Appointment by the President does not mean that every appointee holds office during presidential pleasure. For example, the CAG has a protected removal procedure.

---

## 9. Legislative Powers

The President:

- Summons and prorogues each House.
- Dissolves the **Lok Sabha**, not the Rajya Sabha.
- Summons a joint sitting where constitutionally permitted.
- Addresses Parliament and sends messages to either House.
- Nominates **12 Rajya Sabha members** from persons with special knowledge or practical experience in:
  - Literature.
  - Science.
  - Art.
  - Social service.
- Promulgates ordinances under Article 123.

### Special Address — Article 87

Required at the commencement of:

- The first session after each general election to the Lok Sabha.
- The first session of each year.

It is **not required at every session**.

### Important Distinctions

- The President summons a joint sitting but does not preside over it.
- The Lok Sabha Speaker normally presides.
- Joint sittings are unavailable for **Money Bills and constitutional amendment Bills**.
- The earlier provision for **Anglo-Indian nomination to the Lok Sabha ceased in 2020**.

### Disqualification of MPs — Article 103

- The President decides specified disqualification questions under Article 102(1).
- The President must obtain the **Election Commission’s opinion and act according to it**.
- Defection cases under the **Tenth Schedule** are ordinarily decided by the Speaker or Chairman.

---

## 10. Assent and Veto Powers

### Bills Passed by Parliament

| Type of Bill | President’s options |
|---|---|
| Ordinary Bill | Assent, withhold assent, or return for reconsideration |
| Money Bill | Assent or withhold assent; cannot return for reconsideration |
| Constitutional amendment Bill duly passed under Article 368 | Must give assent |

### Types of Veto

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absolute veto | Withholding assent |
| Suspensive veto | Returning a Bill for reconsideration |
| Pocket veto | Keeping a Bill pending without a decision; the conventional term used in polity |
| Qualified veto | A veto requiring a higher legislative majority to override; not provided for the Indian President |

### Re-passed Parliamentary Bill — Article 111

- Parliament may pass a returned ordinary Bill again **with or without amendments**.
- The President must then assent.
- No additional special majority is required merely because the President returned it.

### Reserved State Bill — Article 201

The President may:

- Give assent.
- Withhold assent.
- Direct the Governor to return a non-Money Bill for reconsideration.

Important differences:

- The State Legislature must reconsider a returned Bill within **six months**.
- Re-passage does not create the same constitutional obligation to assent that Article 111 creates for a parliamentary Bill.

> The six-month period concerns the State Legislature’s reconsideration, not the President’s initial decision.

---

## 11. Ordinance-Making Power — Article 123

### Conditions

An ordinance may be promulgated when:

- At least **one House of Parliament is not in session**; and
- Circumstances require **immediate action**.

It cannot be promulgated while both Houses are in session.

### Features

- Has the same force and effect as an Act of Parliament while operative.
- Must be laid before **both Houses**.
- Is subject to Parliament’s legislative competence and constitutional limitations.
- Can amend an ordinary law.
- Cannot amend the Constitution.
- Does not require a National Emergency.
- Is issued on ministerial advice, not as an independent personal legislative power.

### Duration

- Ceases to operate **six weeks after Parliament reassembles**, unless it ceases earlier.
- If the Houses reassemble on different dates, calculate six weeks from the **later date**.
- Can cease earlier if both Houses pass disapproval resolutions.
- May be withdrawn by the President.

> An ordinance does not automatically become an Act. Parliament must enact legislation to replace it.

---

## 12. Financial Powers

The President:

- Causes the **Annual Financial Statement**, or Union Budget, to be laid before Parliament.
- Recommends the introduction of Money Bills.
- Recommends demands for grants.
- Constitutes the **Finance Commission every fifth year or earlier if necessary**.
- Has the Contingency Fund of India placed at the President’s disposal for advances towards unforeseen expenditure, pending parliamentary authorisation.

---

## 13. Judicial and Clemency Powers

### Judicial Functions

- Appoints Supreme Court and High Court judges through the applicable constitutional process.
- May seek the Supreme Court’s advisory opinion under **Article 143**.
- Under Article 143(1), the Court may decline to answer a reference.

### Clemency — Article 72

The President’s power extends to:

1. Punishment or sentence imposed by a **court martial**.
2. Offences against laws relating to matters within the **Union’s executive power**.
3. **All cases involving a death sentence**.

### Forms of Clemency

| Form | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pardon | Removes the punishment and associated penal consequences |
| Commutation | Substitutes a lighter form of punishment |
| Remission | Reduces the period of punishment without changing its character |
| Respite | Grants a lesser sentence because of special circumstances, such as pregnancy |
| Reprieve | Temporarily stays execution of a sentence, especially a death sentence |

Examples:

- Death sentence changed to imprisonment: **Commutation**.
- Ten years’ rigorous imprisonment reduced to five years’ rigorous imprisonment: **Remission**.
- Execution temporarily postponed: **Reprieve**.

Clemency is an **executive power**, exercised on ministerial advice. It is not an ordinary judicial appeal.

---

## 14. Military, Diplomatic and Emergency Powers

### Military and Diplomatic Position

- The President is the **Supreme Commander of the Defence Forces**.
- Exercise of supreme command is regulated by law.
- Receives foreign diplomatic representatives and appoints Indian diplomatic representatives.
- International agreements are concluded on behalf of the Union through the constitutional executive machinery.

### Emergencies

| Emergency | Article | Basic ground |
|---|---|---|
| National Emergency | 352 | War, external aggression or armed rebellion |
| President’s Rule | 356 | Failure of constitutional machinery in a state |
| Financial Emergency | 360 | Threat to financial stability or credit |

Important distinctions:

- Article 352 requires the **Union Cabinet’s decision in writing**.
- Article 356 may be invoked on a Governor’s report **or otherwise**.
- Presidential proclamations remain subject to the applicable constitutional requirements for parliamentary approval.

---

## 15. Immunities — Article 361

During the President’s term:

- Criminal proceedings cannot be instituted or continued against the President in any court.
- No court process for arrest or imprisonment may be issued.
- Civil proceedings concerning personal acts may be instituted after the prescribed **two-month notice**.

The President is not answerable to a court for exercising official powers and duties.

However:

- Governmental actions remain subject to constitutional judicial review.
- These immunities do not prevent **impeachment**.

---

## 16. High-Yield Prelims Revision

- **Minimum age:** 35 years.
- **Qualification:** Eligibility for election to the Lok Sabha.
- **Term:** Five years from entering office.
- **Resignation:** Addressed to the Vice-President.
- **Re-election:** No constitutional term limit.
- **Election disputes:** Supreme Court.
- **Impeachment:** Either House; two-thirds of total membership in each House.
- **Nominated MPs:** Excluded from election; included in impeachment.
- **MLAs:** Included in election where eligible; excluded from impeachment.
- **Ministerial advice:** Reconsideration permitted; reconsidered advice binding.
- **Money Bill:** Cannot be returned.
- **Constitutional amendment Bill:** Assent mandatory when duly passed.
- **Ordinance:** At least one House must be out of session.
- **Ordinance expiry:** Six weeks from reassembly; use the later date if dates differ.
- **Finance Commission:** Every five years or earlier.
- **Clemency:** Includes court-martial sentences and all death-sentence cases.
- **President:** Part of Parliament, but not a member of either House.

### President's Election Method — Article 55

- **Proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.**

- **Secret ballot.**

- Electors indicate preferences among candidates.

- The result depends on the **value of votes**, not merely the number of electors supporting a candidate.

### Value of Votes

| Elector | Basis of vote value |
|---|---|
| Elected MLA | Relevant state population and number of elected Assembly members |
| Elected MP | Total value of votes assigned to elected MLAs divided by the total number of elected MPs |

- All elected MLAs **within the same state** have equal vote value.

- MLA vote values may differ **between states**.

- All elected MPs have equal vote value, irrespective of their House or state.

The basic calculation for an MLA is:

$$

\text{Value of an MLA's vote}

=

\frac{\text{Relevant population of the state}}

{\text{Number of elected MLAs}\times 1000}

$$

- Apply the constitutional rounding rule: a remainder of **500 or more** in the population-per-MLA calculation adds one vote.

- For an MP’s vote value, a fraction **exceeding one-half** is rounded up; other fractions are disregarded.

### Population Basis

- The **1971 Census** population remains the basis until the relevant figures of the **first census taken after 2026** are published.

- The passage of the year 2026 alone does not change the population basis.

### Winning Quota: What Majority Is Required?

The quota is based on the **total value of valid votes actually cast**, rather than the total number of members in the electoral college.

$$
\text{Quota} = \left\lfloor \frac{\text{Total value of valid votes cast}}{2} \right\rfloor + 1
$$

The floor brackets mean **discard the fractional remainder** before adding one.

| Total value of valid votes cast | Quota |
|---|---:|
| 1,000 | 501 |
| 1,001 | 501 |

- The quota is the smallest whole number greater than half the total valid vote value polled.
- Invalid ballots and votes not cast are excluded from its calculation.
- The quota is calculated at the first count; it is not reduced merely because some ballots later become exhausted.
- A candidate can reach the quota through **first-preference votes together with transferred votes**.
- Receiving first-preference support from more than half of all electoral-college members is **not a requirement**.

### Understanding the Single Transferable Vote

Each elector casts **one ballot** and may rank the candidates in order of preference.

| Candidate | Preference marked on a hypothetical ballot |
|---|---|
| X | 1 — first choice |
| Y | 2 — second choice |
| Z | 3 — third choice |

- **First preference is compulsory** for a valid ballot.
- Second and subsequent preferences are **optional**.
- Ranking several candidates does not give them simultaneous votes.
- The ballot initially counts for the first choice. If that candidate is eliminated, it may transfer to the next preferred candidate still in the contest.
- The elector does not vote again during subsequent counting rounds.

**“Single”** refers to one transferable vote, despite the elector marking several preferences. **“Transferable”** means that the ballot can move to another candidate according to those preferences. The vote still carries its prescribed **value**, which need not be one.

### How Counting Works

1. Separate valid ballots from invalid ballots.
2. Credit each valid ballot's value to its first-preference candidate and determine the quota.
3. If a candidate's total equals or exceeds the quota, declare that candidate elected.
4. If nobody reaches the quota, eliminate the candidate with the **lowest total vote value**.
5. Transfer that candidate's ballots to continuing candidates according to the **next available preferences**, retaining their original vote values.
6. Repeat the process as necessary.

**Exhausted ballots:** A ballot with no further usable preference cannot be transferred and takes no further part in counting.

**Technical qualification:** If only one continuing candidate remains after the others have been excluded, that candidate is declared elected even without reaching the quota.

### Worked Example: Winning Through Transferred Preferences

Assume the total valid vote value is **1,000**. The quota is **501**.

| Candidate | Vote value from first preferences |
|---|---:|
| X | 450 |
| Y | 350 |
| Z | 200 |
| **Total** | **1,000** |

Nobody reaches the quota. Z has the lowest total and is eliminated.

Suppose Z's ballots transfer **160** in vote value to Y and **40** to X, according to the next preferences marked.

| Candidate | Value after transfer | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| X | 450 + 40 = **490** | Below quota |
| Y | 350 + 160 = **510** | **Elected** |

Y wins despite starting with fewer first-preference votes by value than X.

> **Prelims distinction:** The highest first-preference total does not necessarily ensure victory. Transferred preferences can change the result.

### Why the Constitution Calls It Proportional Representation

**Article 55(3)** expressly uses the phrase **proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote**. Retain that constitutional wording for Prelims.

Since only **one office** is being filled, the presidential election does not distribute several seats among parties in proportion to their votes. Its practical counting mechanism is preferential voting with elimination and transfer.
