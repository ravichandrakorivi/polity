# Governor of a State

## 1. Constitutional position of the Governor

The Governor is the constitutional head of a State and ordinarily exercises executive functions on the aid and advice of the State Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister.

India follows a parliamentary system at both Union and State levels.

- Governor: Constitutional head of the State.
- Chief Minister: Head of the elected State Government.
- State Council of Ministers: Exercises political executive authority and is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
- President: Appoints the Governor.

Article 153: There shall be a Governor for each State. The same person may be appointed Governor of two or more States.

## 2. Appointment, qualifications and tenure

### 2.1 Appointment — Article 155

- The Governor is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal.
- The Governor is not directly or indirectly elected.
- The President acts within the Union's constitutional parliamentary framework.

### 2.2 Qualifications — Article 157

A person must:

1. Be a citizen of India.
2. Have completed 35 years of age.

The Constitution does not require the Governor to be a resident of the State concerned.

### 2.3 Conditions of office — Article 158

- Cannot be a member of Parliament or a State Legislature.
- If a sitting MP or MLA is appointed Governor, the legislative seat is deemed vacated upon entering office.
- Cannot hold any other office of profit.
- Entitled to official residence without payment of rent.
- Emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during the term.
- When the same person serves as Governor of multiple States, emoluments and allowances are allocated among those States as determined by the President.

### 2.4 Tenure — Article 156

- Normal tenure: Five years from entering office.
- Holds office during the pleasure of the President.
- May resign by writing addressed to the President.
- May continue beyond five years until a successor enters office.
- Can be removed before five years; no parliamentary impeachment procedure is prescribed.

In B. P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010), the Supreme Court held that removal cannot be arbitrary, capricious or unreasonable, although the President is not required to give reasons in the removal order.

### 2.5 Oath — Article 159

The Governor takes an oath to:

- Faithfully execute the office.
- Preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law.
- Devote himself or herself to the service and well-being of the people of the State.

Administered by: Chief Justice of the concerned High Court or, in the Chief Justice's absence, the senior-most available judge of that High Court.

## 3. Executive powers

The executive power of the State is vested in the Governor under Article 154 and is ordinarily exercised through officers subordinate to the Governor in accordance with the Constitution.

### 3.1 Appointments

The Governor appoints:

- Chief Minister.
- Other State ministers, on the Chief Minister's advice.
- Advocate General of the State.
- Chairperson and members of the State Public Service Commission.
- State Election Commissioner, subject to applicable law.
- State Finance Commission, as required under Article 243-I.

The Governor also participates in certain university appointments where the relevant State law assigns the Governor a role.

Important distinction: High Court judges are appointed by the President, not the Governor. The Governor is consulted under Article 217.

### 3.2 Chief Minister and Council of Ministers

Under Article 164:

- The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor.
- Other ministers are appointed on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- Ministers hold office during the Governor's pleasure, interpreted within the framework of responsible parliamentary government.
- The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the State Legislative Assembly, not the Legislative Council.

In a hung Assembly: The Governor may exercise constitutional judgment in appointing the person most likely to command Assembly confidence. Majority support should ordinarily be determined through a floor test.

### 3.3 Duties of Chief Minister — Article 167

The Chief Minister must:

- Communicate Council of Ministers' decisions concerning State administration and legislative proposals.
- Furnish information requested by the Governor.
- Submit a matter decided by an individual minister for consideration by the Council of Ministers if the Governor so requires.

## 4. Legislative powers

### 4.1 Summoning, prorogation and dissolution — Article 174

The Governor formally:

- Summons the State Legislature.
- Prorogues its House or Houses.
- Dissolves the Legislative Assembly.

Ordinarily, these powers are exercised on ministerial advice, subject to limited constitutional exceptions.

Six-month rule: Not more than six months may intervene between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next session of the State Legislature.

The Legislative Council is a continuing House and is not subject to dissolution.

### 4.2 Address and messages

| Article | Power                                                                                                           |
| ------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 175     | Address either House or both Houses assembled together; send messages                                           |
| 176     | Special address at the first session after each general Assembly election and at the first session each year    |
| 177     | Rights of ministers and Advocate General to participate in legislative proceedings, subject to the Constitution |

### 4.3 Nomination to the Legislative Council — Article 171

Where a State has a Legislative Council, the Governor nominates approximately one-sixth of its members.

Nominees must possess special knowledge or practical experience in:

- Literature
- Science
- Art
- Cooperative movement
- Social service

UPSC trap: The Governor does not currently have an operative power to nominate an Anglo-Indian member to the Legislative Assembly. This special representation ceased in 2020 following the constitutional time limit under Article 334.

## 5. Governor's assent to State Bills — Article 200

When a Bill passed by the State Legislature is presented to the Governor, the constitutional options are:

1. Give assent.
2. Withhold assent.
3. Reserve the Bill for the consideration of the President.
4. In the case of a Bill other than a Money Bill, return it with a message requesting reconsideration.

If a returned Bill is passed again and presented, Article 200 states that the Governor shall not withhold assent.

### 5.1 Reservation for President

The Governor must reserve a State Bill for the President's consideration if, in the Governor's opinion, the Bill would seriously weaken the powers of the High Court and endanger its constitutional position.

Other Bills may be reserved in constitutionally permitted circumstances.

### 5.2 President's consideration — Article 201

For a Bill reserved by the Governor, the President may:

- Assent to the Bill.
- Withhold assent.
- In the case of a non-Money Bill, direct the Governor to return it for reconsideration.

The Legislature must reconsider such a returned Bill within six months of receiving the message. Unlike Article 200, Article 201 does not make presidential assent mandatory merely because the State Legislature passes the Bill again.

### 5.3 Important judicial developments

The interpretation of Articles 200 and 201 has been the subject of major Supreme Court decisions and a Presidential Reference in 2025. For Prelims, distinguish the express constitutional text from judicially developed rules concerning delay, reconsideration and the Governor's discretion. The constitutional text does not specify a numerical deadline for every assent decision.

### 5.4 Supreme Court's 2025 Presidential Reference — important update

On 20 November 2025, a five-judge Constitution Bench clarified the operation of Articles 200 and 201.

- The Governor's three substantive choices are assent, reservation for the President, and withholding assent accompanied by return of the Bill in accordance with Article 200.
- Courts cannot prescribe generally binding numerical timelines for assent decisions where the Constitution has not prescribed them.
- Courts cannot declare that a Bill has received deemed assent merely because of delay.
- Prolonged, unexplained inaction is not beyond judicial scrutiny; limited judicial directions may be available without dictating the substantive decision.
- The Court clarified that its earlier April 2025 judgment prescribing timelines was erroneous on that point.

Prelims trap: Do not use the April 2025 judicially prescribed one-month and three-month deadlines as the current governing position.

## 6. Ordinance-making power — Article 213

The Governor may promulgate Ordinances when immediate legislative action is necessary and the constitutional conditions are satisfied.

### 6.1 Conditions

- In a unicameral State, the Legislative Assembly must not be in session.
- In a bicameral State, an Ordinance may be promulgated when both Houses are not simultaneously in session.
- The Governor must be satisfied that circumstances require immediate action.
- The power is ordinarily exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.

### 6.2 Duration

- An Ordinance has the same force and effect as a State law.
- It must be laid before the State Legislature.
- It ordinarily ceases to operate six weeks after the Legislature reassembles, unless replaced by legislation.
- Where the Houses reassemble on different dates, the six weeks are counted from the later date.
- The Governor may withdraw an Ordinance earlier.

### 6.3 When President's instructions are required

The Governor cannot promulgate an Ordinance without instructions from the President if:

1. A Bill containing the same provisions would require the President's previous sanction for introduction.
2. The Governor would consider it necessary to reserve such a Bill for the President.
3. A State Act containing those provisions would be invalid without reservation and presidential assent.

UPSC trap: The Governor's ordinance-making power is not unlimited. It is subject to legislative competence and constitutional restrictions.

## 7. Financial powers

The Governor performs important constitutional functions relating to State finances.

| Power                                                                                  | Constitutional provision |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Causes the Annual Financial Statement (State Budget) to be laid before the Legislature | Article 202              |
| Recommendation for introduction of specified Financial Bills                           | Article 207              |
| Recommendation for Demands for Grants                                                  | Article 203              |
| Supplementary, additional and excess grants                                            | Article 205              |
| Votes on account, votes of credit and exceptional grants                               | Article 206              |
| State Contingency Fund placed at Governor's disposal                                   | Article 267(2)           |
| Constitutes State Finance Commission every five years                                  | Article 243-I            |

### Important points

- A Money Bill cannot be introduced in a State Legislative Assembly without the Governor's recommendation.
- The Governor does not personally vote on the Budget.
- The State Legislative Assembly votes on Demands for Grants.
- Charged expenditure is not submitted to vote but remains subject to the appropriation process.
- The Governor's recommendation and the Governor's assent are distinct constitutional steps.

## 8. Judicial powers

### 8.1 Pardoning power — Article 161

The Governor can grant pardons, reprieves, respites or remissions of punishment, and suspend, remit or commute sentences for offences against laws relating to matters within the State's executive power.

### 8.2 Governor versus President: Clemency powers

| Feature                                          | President — Article 72               | Governor — Article 161                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Court-martial cases                              | Yes                                  | No                                                                              |
| Offences within Union executive power            | Yes                                  | Not generally                                                                   |
| Offences within State executive power            | Not the ordinary basis of Article 72 | Yes                                                                             |
| Death sentence                                   | Can pardon                           | Can suspend, remit or commute; cannot pardon a death sentence under Article 161 |
| Exercises power ordinarily on ministerial advice | Yes                                  | Yes                                                                             |

Important distinction: The Governor may commute a death sentence to a lesser punishment but does not possess the President's express constitutional power to pardon a death sentence.

### 8.3 Other judicial-related functions

- Consulted in the appointment of High Court judges under Article 217.
- Appoints district judges in consultation with the High Court under Article 233.
- Appointments of persons other than district judges to the State judicial service are governed by Article 234, involving consultation with the State Public Service Commission and High Court.

## 9. Discretionary powers of the Governor

Article 163 provides for a Council of Ministers to aid and advise the Governor, except where the Constitution requires the Governor to exercise functions in discretion.

### 9.1 Important situations

| Situation                                           | Governor's role                                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No party or coalition has a clear Assembly majority | Appoints the person likely to command Assembly confidence                 |
| Doubt about majority support                        | May require a floor test in constitutionally justified circumstances      |
| Constitutional breakdown in the State               | May report to the President under Article 356                             |
| Reservation of Bills                                | Exercises powers under Article 200 within constitutional limits           |
| Special responsibilities in certain States          | Exercises functions according to the applicable constitutional provisions |

The Governor does not possess a general, unlimited discretionary power.

### 9.2 Floor test

A floor test determines whether the government commands the confidence of the elected Legislative Assembly.

The Governor cannot ordinarily replace the Assembly's assessment of majority support with a personal assessment.

### 9.3 Important Supreme Court cases

| Case                                                                 | Principle                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)                             | Governor ordinarily acts on ministerial aid and advice                                                       |
| S. R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)                                | Majority support should ordinarily be tested on the floor of the House; Article 356 is judicially reviewable |
| Rameshwar Prasad v. Union of India (2006)                            | Improper dissolution of a State Assembly can be judicially reviewed                                          |
| B. P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010)                               | Removal of Governor cannot be arbitrary                                                                      |
| Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016)                                 | Governor's discretion concerning legislative proceedings is constitutionally limited                         |
| Subhash Desai v. Principal Secretary, Governor of Maharashtra (2023) | Floor-test directions require objective material indicating loss of majority                                 |

## 10. Governor and President's Rule — Article 356

If the President is satisfied that the government of a State cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution, a proclamation under Article 356 may be issued.

- The Governor may submit a report recommending President's Rule.
- The President may act with or without a Governor's report.
- The proclamation requires approval by both Houses of Parliament within two months, subject to constitutional exceptions.
- Approval ordinarily requires a simple majority in each House.
- Once approved, it normally operates for six months at a time, subject to constitutional limits and conditions for extension.

UPSC trap: The Governor recommends or reports; the Governor does not personally impose President's Rule.

## 11. Constitutional immunity — Article 361

The Governor enjoys certain constitutional protections during the term of office.

- Not answerable to any court for the exercise and performance of the powers and duties of office, or acts purportedly done in their exercise.
- No criminal proceedings may be instituted or continued against the Governor during the term.
- No process for arrest or imprisonment may issue from any court during the term.
- Civil proceedings concerning personal acts may be instituted during the term only subject to the constitutional two-month prior-notice requirement.

Important: Article 361 does not place all official actions beyond judicial review. Courts may examine the legality of government or constitutional action without making the Governor personally answerable.

## 13. UPSC Prelims revision checklist

1. Appointment: Governor is appointed, not elected.
2. Qualifications: Citizenship and minimum age of 35 years; State residency is not mandatory.
3. Tenure: Five years is the normal term, subject to President's pleasure.
4. Removal: No impeachment process for a Governor.
5. Executive: Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor; other ministers are appointed on the Chief Minister's advice.
6. Collective responsibility: State Council of Ministers is responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
7. Legislative Council: Governor nominates approximately one-sixth of members from constitutionally specified fields.
8. Bills: Article 200 concerns the Governor; Article 201 concerns the President's consideration of reserved State Bills.
9. Ordinances: Article 213; six-week expiry rule after legislative reassembly.
10. Financial powers: Money Bills require the Governor's recommendation for introduction.
11. Clemency: Governor cannot pardon a death sentence under Article 161 but can commute it.
12. Discretion: Constitutional discretion is limited, not general.
13. Floor test: Legislative Assembly is the appropriate forum for testing majority.
14. President's Rule: Governor's report is not an indispensable prerequisite.
15. Immunity: Article 361 provides personal constitutional protection, not blanket immunity from judicial review of official action.
16. 2025 judicial development: No judicially prescribed universal assent deadlines or deemed assent under Articles 200–201.

### Q20. Assertion (A): The Governor must obtain the approval of the State Legislative Council before dissolving the Legislative Assembly.

### Reason (R): The State Legislative Council is a continuing House and is not subject to dissolution.
