
# Vice-President of India

## 1. Constitutional Position

- **Article 63:** There shall be a Vice-President of India.
- The Vice-President occupies the **second-highest constitutional office** and ranks immediately after the President in the Order of Precedence.
- The office is modelled broadly on the American vice-presidency.
- Its two principal constitutional functions are:
  1. Serving as **ex officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**.
  2. Acting as President or discharging presidential functions in the circumstances specified in Article 65.

> **Ex officio** means holding an office by virtue of holding another office. The Vice-President automatically becomes Rajya Sabha Chairman without a separate election to that position.

---

## 2. Election — Article 66

### Electoral College

The Vice-President is indirectly elected by an electoral college consisting of **members of both Houses of Parliament**, including nominated members.

| Category | Participates in the election? |
|---|---|
| Elected Lok Sabha members | Yes |
| Elected Rajya Sabha members | Yes |
| Nominated Rajya Sabha members | Yes |
| Members of State Legislative Assemblies | No |
| Members of State Legislative Councils | No |
| Members of Delhi and Puducherry Legislative Assemblies | No |

### Method of Election

- **Proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.**
- **Secret ballot.**
- Each elector’s vote has an equal value of **one**.
- Electors indicate preferences among candidates.
- Marking the **first preference is compulsory**; subsequent preferences are optional.
- The **Election Commission of India** conducts the election.

> Unlike the presidential election, population-based weighting of votes does not apply.

### Nomination Requirements

- At least **20 electors as proposers**.
- At least **20 electors as seconders**.
- Security deposit: **₹15,000**.

### Eleventh Constitutional Amendment, 1961

- Originally, the Vice-President was to be elected by members of both Houses assembled at a **joint meeting**.
- The **11th Amendment** replaced this with an electoral college consisting of members of both Houses.
- A joint meeting of Parliament is therefore **not required** for the election.

### Why Are MLAs Excluded?

- The Vice-President’s regular function is to preside over the Rajya Sabha.
- Performing presidential functions is a temporary responsibility in specified circumstances.
- The Constitution therefore provides a parliamentary electoral college without including State Legislatures.

---

## 3. Qualifications

A candidate must:

1. Be an **Indian citizen**.
2. Have completed **35 years of age**.
3. Be qualified for election as a member of the **Rajya Sabha**.
4. Not hold a disqualifying **office of profit**.

For this purpose, holding any of the following offices does not itself cause disqualification:

- President.
- Vice-President.
- Governor.
- Union or State Minister.

### Important Distinctions

- A candidate need not already be a Member of Parliament.
- Eligibility for election to the Rajya Sabha does not reduce the minimum age for Vice-President to 30.
- **Vice-President:** Must be qualified for election to the Rajya Sabha.
- **President:** Must be qualified for election to the Lok Sabha.

---

## 4. Conditions of Office

- The Vice-President cannot be a member of Parliament or a State Legislature.
- If a legislator is elected Vice-President, the legislative seat is deemed vacant on **entering vice-presidential office**.
- The seat is not vacated merely on nomination or declaration of the election result.
- The Vice-President cannot hold another office of profit.

> Although the Vice-President chairs the Rajya Sabha, the Vice-President is **not a member of that House**.

---

## 5. Oath or Affirmation — Article 69

The oath is made before:

- The **President**; or
- A person appointed by the President for that purpose.

The Vice-President undertakes to:

- Bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India.
- Faithfully discharge the duties of the office.

### Distinction from the President’s Oath

| Office | Main undertaking |
|---|---|
| Vice-President | Allegiance to the Constitution and faithful discharge of duties |
| President | Faithful execution of office; preservation, protection and defence of the Constitution and law; service and well-being of the people |

---

## 6. Term, Resignation and Re-election — Article 67

| Matter | Provision |
|---|---|
| Normal term | Five years from entering office |
| Resignation addressed to | President |
| Re-election | Permitted; no constitutional limit on number of terms |
| Successor has not entered office | Incumbent continues beyond the five-year term |
| Effect of Lok Sabha dissolution | Does not terminate the Vice-President’s tenure |

> The term begins on **entering office**, not on the date of election.

---

## 7. Vacancy and Timing of Election — Article 68

A vacancy may arise through:

- Expiry of the term.
- Resignation.
- Removal.
- Death.
- Other circumstances, such as the election being declared void.

### Election Requirements

| Situation | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Normal expiry of the term | Election must be completed before the term expires |
| Casual vacancy due to death, resignation, removal or otherwise | Election must be held as soon as possible |
| Person elected to fill a casual vacancy | Receives a full five-year term from entering office |

### President versus Vice-President

- **Presidential casual vacancy:** Election must be held within **six months**.
- **Vice-presidential casual vacancy:** Election must be held **as soon as possible**; Article 68 does not prescribe the same six-month outer limit.

### Who Performs the Duties During a Vacancy?

- The **Deputy Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** performs the Chairman’s duties under Article 91.
- The Deputy Chairman does **not become acting Vice-President**.
- Performing the Chairman’s duties does not confer the Vice-President’s power to act as President.

---

## 8. Removal — Article 67

The Vice-President is removed through a **parliamentary resolution**, not through the impeachment procedure applicable to the President.

### Procedure

1. The resolution must originate in the **Rajya Sabha**.
2. At least **14 days’ notice** must be given of the intention to move it.
3. The Rajya Sabha must pass it by a **majority of all the then members** (All existing members of the House => Effective majority: total strength minus vacant seats).
4. The Lok Sabha must agree to it by a **simple majority of members present and voting**.

### Grounds for Removal

- The Constitution does not prescribe specific grounds.
- It does not require a charge of **“violation of the Constitution”**, which applies to presidential impeachment.

### Meaning of Effective Majority

A majority of all the then members is commonly called an **effective majority**.

- Vacant seats are excluded.
- Members who are absent or abstain are not excluded from the then membership.

**Example:**

| Particular | Number |
|---|---:|
| Total membership | 245 |
| Vacant seats | 5 |
| Then membership | 240 |
| Minimum affirmative votes required | 121 |

> Use **effective majority**, not absolute majority of the sanctioned strength, for removal in the Rajya Sabha.

| Type of majority | Votes required |
|---|---|
| **Simple majority** | More than half of members present and voting |
| **Effective majority** | More than half of total membership **excluding vacant seats** |
| **Absolute majority** | More than half of total membership |
| **Special majority under Article 61** | **At least two-thirds of total membership** |

---

## 9. Rights During Removal Proceedings — Article 92

While a resolution for the Vice-President’s removal is under consideration in the Rajya Sabha, the Vice-President:

- **Cannot preside**.
- May speak and participate.
- **Cannot vote**, even in the event of a tie.
- Cannot vote on any other matter during those proceedings.

> Participation is allowed; presiding and voting are prohibited.

---

## 10. Chairman of the Rajya Sabha

### Principal Functions

As Chairman, the Vice-President:

- Presides over proceedings.
- Maintains order and decorum.
- Applies and interprets the House’s procedural rules.
- Decides disqualification questions concerning Rajya Sabha members under the **Tenth Schedule**, subject to judicial review.

### Voting Power — Article 100

- Does not vote in the first instance.
- Exercises a **casting vote when votes are equally divided**.
- This power is unavailable during proceedings considering the Vice-President’s removal.

### Chairman and Deputy Chairman Compared

| Feature | Chairman | Deputy Chairman |
|---|---|---|
| How office is obtained | Vice-President serves ex officio | Elected by the Rajya Sabha |
| Rajya Sabha membership | Not a member | Must be a member |
| Performs Chairman’s duties during vacancy or absence | — | Yes, under Article 91 |
| Becomes Vice-President by performing Chairman’s duties | — | No |

### Limits to Remember

- The Rajya Sabha Chairman does not normally preside over a joint sitting of Parliament; the **Lok Sabha Speaker** does.
- Certification of a Bill as a **Money Bill** belongs to the Lok Sabha Speaker, not the Rajya Sabha Chairman.

---

## 11. Acting as President — Article 65

### A. Vacancy in the President’s Office

When the presidential office becomes vacant through death, resignation, removal or otherwise:

- The Vice-President **acts as President**.
- This continues until the newly elected President enters office.
- The presidential election must be held within **six months** of the vacancy.
- The Vice-President does not automatically become President for the predecessor’s remaining term.

### B. Temporary Inability of the President

When the President cannot discharge duties because of illness, absence or another cause:

- The Vice-President discharges presidential functions.
- This continues until the President resumes duties.
- Temporary inability does not itself create a vacancy or require a presidential election.

> The six-month election rule for a presidential vacancy should not be applied indiscriminately to temporary presidential inability.

### Powers and Entitlements

While acting as President or discharging presidential functions, the Vice-President:

- Has the **powers and immunities of the President**.
- Receives the applicable presidential emoluments, allowances and privileges.
- Does **not perform the duties of Rajya Sabha Chairman**.
- Does **not receive the Chairman’s salary and allowances**.

The Vice-President need not resign from the vice-presidential office to perform these functions.

### Oath for Presidential Functions

- The oath under Article 60 applies to a person acting as President or discharging presidential functions.
- Thus, the Vice-President’s oath under Article 69 does not replace the presidential oath required for this role.

---

## 12. Salary and Allowances

- The Vice-President draws the regular salary in the capacity of **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha**.
- There is no additional separate salary merely for holding the vice-presidential office.
- During the performance of presidential functions, presidential entitlements replace the Chairman’s salary and allowances.

---

## 13. Election Disputes — Article 71

- Decided by the **Supreme Court**, whose decision is final.
- The election cannot be challenged merely because a vacancy existed in the electoral college.
- If the election is declared void, official acts performed on or before the Court’s decision are not invalidated merely because of that declaration.

---

## 14. Indian and American Vice-Presidents

| Feature | India | United States |
|---|---|---|
| Upper House role | Chairman of Rajya Sabha | President of the Senate |
| On the President’s death in office | Acts as President temporarily | Becomes President |
| Tenure following such an event | Until the newly elected President enters office | Remainder of the predecessor’s term |

> **India:** Temporary acting arrangement.  
> **United States:** Succession to the presidency.

| Feature | President | Vice-President |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum age | 35 years | 35 years |
| Parliamentary qualification | Eligible for Lok Sabha | Eligible for Rajya Sabha |
| Electoral college | Elected MPs and eligible elected MLAs | Members of both Houses of Parliament |
| Nominated MPs participate | No | Yes |
| MLAs participate | Yes, where constitutionally eligible | No |
| Value of each MP’s vote | Calculated under Article 55 | One |
| Proposers and seconders | 50 each | 20 each |
| Security deposit | ₹15,000 | ₹15,000 |
| Normal term | Five years | Five years |
| Resignation addressed to | Vice-President | President |
| Removal initiated in | Either House | Rajya Sabha only |
| Ground for removal | Violation of the Constitution | No specific ground prescribed |
| Removal majority | Two-thirds of total membership in each House | Effective majority in Rajya Sabha; simple majority in Lok Sabha |
| Casual vacancy election | Within six months | As soon as possible |
| Term after election to casual vacancy | Full five years | Full five years |

## 18. Quick Prelims Revision

- **Nominated MPs participate; MLAs do not.**
- **Vote value: one for every elector.**
- **Minimum age: 35 years.**
- **Qualification: eligibility for Rajya Sabha, not existing membership.**
- **Nomination: 20 proposers and 20 seconders.**
- **Resignation: addressed to the President.**
- **Removal: initiated only in the Rajya Sabha.**
- **Rajya Sabha: effective majority; Lok Sabha: simple majority.**
- **Removal notice: at least 14 days.**
- **Casual vacancy: election as soon as possible; no prescribed six-month outer limit.**
- **Deputy Chairman does not become acting Vice-President.**
- **Vice-President is not a Rajya Sabha member.**
- **Ordinary proceedings: casting vote in a tie.**
- **Own removal proceedings: no vote at all.**
- **Acting as President: no simultaneous Chairman’s duties or Chairman’s salary.**
- **11th Amendment, 1961: removed the joint-meeting requirement for election.**
