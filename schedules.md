# Schedules of the Constitution of India

## 1. Introduction

The Constitution of India contains 12 Schedules, which provide detailed provisions relating to the subjects mentioned in various constitutional articles.

- The Constitution originally contained 8 Schedules.
- Four Schedules were added subsequently through constitutional amendments.
- Schedules form an integral part of the Constitution and have constitutional authority.
- A Schedule may contain lists, forms, rules, allocations or other detailed provisions.

### All 12 Schedules at a glance

| Schedule | Subject                                                                        |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| First    | States and Union Territories and their territories                             |
| Second   | Emoluments, allowances and privileges of specified constitutional authorities  |
| Third    | Forms of oaths and affirmations                                                |
| Fourth   | Allocation of Rajya Sabha seats to States and Union Territories                |
| Fifth    | Administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes             |
| Sixth    | Administration of tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram        |
| Seventh  | Union List, State List and Concurrent List                                     |
| Eighth   | Recognised languages listed in the Constitution                                |
| Ninth    | Certain laws protected under Article 31B, subject to judicial review           |
| Tenth    | Anti-Defection Law                                                             |
| Eleventh | Powers, authority and responsibilities that may be entrusted to Panchayats     |
| Twelfth  | Powers, authority and responsibilities that may be entrusted to Municipalities |

## 2. First Schedule — States and Union Territories

Relevant Articles: 1 and 4

The First Schedule specifies:

- The names of the States of India and their territories.
- The names of the Union Territories and their territorial extent.

India currently consists of 28 States and 8 Union Territories.

### Important points

- Article 1 describes India as a Union of States.
- Parliament may create new States or alter the areas, boundaries or names of existing States under Article 3.
- A law made under Articles 2 or 3 may amend the First and Fourth Schedules as necessary.
- Such changes are not treated as constitutional amendments under Article 368.

Example: Reorganisation of a State may require corresponding changes to the First Schedule.

UPSC trap: Not every change to a constitutional Schedule requires the special-majority amendment procedure under Article 368.

## 3. Second Schedule — Emoluments and allowances

Relevant Articles: 59, 65, 75, 97, 125, 148, 158, 164, 186 and 221

The Second Schedule contains provisions relating to the emoluments, allowances and privileges of certain constitutional authorities.

These include:

- President and Governors.
- Presiding officers of Parliament and State Legislatures.
- Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts.
- Comptroller and Auditor General of India.

The Schedule also contains provisions relevant to ministers' allowances where applicable under the constitutional framework.

### Important distinction

The Second Schedule does not independently fix every current salary of all these authorities. Parliament or State Legislatures may determine relevant salaries and allowances through laws, as permitted by the Constitution.

UPSC trap: The Second Schedule is not concerned with the allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha; that is the Fourth Schedule.

## 4. Third Schedule — Forms of oaths and affirmations

Relevant Articles: 75, 84, 99, 124, 148, 164, 173, 188 and 219

The Third Schedule prescribes forms of oaths or affirmations for:

- Union Ministers.
- Candidates for Parliament.
- Members of Parliament.
- Judges of the Supreme Court.
- Comptroller and Auditor General.
- State Ministers.
- Candidates for State Legislatures.
- Members of State Legislatures.
- Judges of High Courts.

### Important distinction

The forms of oath for the President, Vice-President and Governors are prescribed directly in the Constitution, rather than in the Third Schedule.

| Authority           | Oath provision |
| ------------------- | -------------- |
| President           | Article 60     |
| Vice-President      | Article 69     |
| Governor            | Article 159    |
| Union Minister      | Third Schedule |
| State Minister      | Third Schedule |
| Supreme Court Judge | Third Schedule |
| High Court Judge    | Third Schedule |

UPSC trap: The President's oath is not prescribed in the Third Schedule.

## 5. Fourth Schedule — Allocation of Rajya Sabha seats

Relevant Articles: 4 and 80

The Fourth Schedule allocates seats in the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) to States and Union Territories.

### Important points

- Rajya Sabha representation is not equal for all States.
- Larger States generally receive more seats than smaller States.
- Representation is based broadly on population, subject to constitutional and statutory arrangements.
- The Fourth Schedule deals with allocated seats, not the nomination of members by the President.

Under Article 80, the President nominates 12 members to the Rajya Sabha from persons having special knowledge or practical experience in literature, science, art and social service.

UPSC trap: The Fourth Schedule concerns Rajya Sabha seat allocation, not Lok Sabha constituency delimitation.

## 6. Fifth Schedule — Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes

Relevant Article: 244(1)

The Fifth Schedule deals with the administration and control of Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes in States other than Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.

### 6.1 Scheduled Areas

Scheduled Areas are areas declared by the President under the Fifth Schedule.

The President may, through the prescribed constitutional process:

- Declare an area to be a Scheduled Area.
- Alter the boundaries of Scheduled Areas.
- Increase or decrease their extent.
- Rescind relevant Scheduled Area orders.

### 6.2 Tribes Advisory Council

The Fifth Schedule provides for a Tribes Advisory Council (TAC):

- In every State having Scheduled Areas.
- In a State having Scheduled Tribes but no Scheduled Areas, if the President so directs.

The TAC has not more than 20 members.

As nearly as may be, three-fourths of its members must be representatives of Scheduled Tribes in the State Legislative Assembly.

Its principal function is to advise on matters concerning the welfare and advancement of Scheduled Tribes referred to it by the Governor.

### 6.3 Special powers of the Governor

The Governor may direct that an Act of Parliament or State Legislature:

- Does not apply to a Scheduled Area; or
- Applies subject to specified exceptions or modifications.

The Governor may also make regulations for the peace and good government of Scheduled Areas, including regulations concerning:

- Transfer of land by or among members of Scheduled Tribes.
- Allotment of land to members of Scheduled Tribes.
- Money-lending to members of Scheduled Tribes.

Important: Regulations made by the Governor under the Fifth Schedule require the President's assent.

### 6.4 Governor's report

The Governor of a State having Scheduled Areas submits a report to the President regarding their administration, annually or whenever required.

The Union executive may issue directions to the State concerning the administration of Scheduled Areas.

UPSC trap: Fifth Schedule administration is not the same as the autonomous district council system under the Sixth Schedule.

## 7. Sixth Schedule — Autonomous tribal areas

Relevant Articles: 244(2) and 275(1)

The Sixth Schedule provides special administrative arrangements for tribal areas in four northeastern States:

1. Assam
2. Meghalaya
3. Tripura
4. Mizoram

Its central feature is the establishment of Autonomous District Councils (ADCs) and, where applicable, Regional Councils.

### 7.1 Autonomous District Councils

- An autonomous district ordinarily has a District Council with a maximum of 30 members.
- Ordinarily, not more than four members are nominated by the Governor, and the remaining members are elected by adult suffrage.
- Special provisions apply to certain councils, including the Bodoland Territorial Council.
- The ordinary term of elected members is five years, subject to constitutional provisions.

### 7.2 Powers of District and Regional Councils

These councils may exercise specified legislative, administrative and judicial powers.

Their legislative powers cover matters such as:

- Allotment, occupation and use of land, subject to constitutional limitations.
- Management of forests other than reserved forests.
- Use of canals and watercourses for agriculture.
- Shifting cultivation.
- Establishment of village or town committees.
- Village administration.
- Inheritance of property.
- Marriage and divorce.
- Social customs.

Council laws on specified subjects require the Governor's assent.

Councils also possess certain powers to establish village courts, manage local institutions and levy specified taxes.

### 7.3 Fifth Schedule versus Sixth Schedule

| Feature            | Fifth Schedule                                                   | Sixth Schedule                            |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Relevant Article   | 244(1)                                                           | 244(2)                                    |
| States covered     | Scheduled Areas in States outside the four Sixth Schedule States | Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram     |
| Main institution   | Tribes Advisory Council                                          | Autonomous District and Regional Councils |
| Main purpose       | Special protection and administration of Scheduled Areas         | Tribal autonomy and self-government       |
| Legislative powers | Special regulatory powers of Governor                            | Specified law-making powers of councils   |
| Judicial powers    | No general autonomous district court system under the Schedule   | Councils may establish specified courts   |

Prelims trap: Nagaland is not one of the four Sixth Schedule States. It has special constitutional protections under Article 371A.

## 8. Seventh Schedule — Division of legislative powers

Relevant Article: 246

The Seventh Schedule contains three legislative lists.

| List                       | Principal law-making authority    | Examples                                               |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| List I — Union List        | Parliament                        | Defence, foreign affairs, currency, railways, banking  |
| List II — State List       | State Legislatures                | Police, public order, public health, agriculture       |
| List III — Concurrent List | Parliament and State Legislatures | Education, forests, marriage and divorce, criminal law |

### 8.1 Union List

Parliament ordinarily has exclusive legislative authority over Union List subjects.

Examples include defence, atomic energy, citizenship, foreign affairs, banking, currency and railways.

### 8.2 State List

State Legislatures ordinarily legislate on State List subjects.

However, Parliament may legislate on State List matters in specified constitutional circumstances, including:

- Article 249 — Rajya Sabha resolution in the national interest.
- Article 250 — During a National Emergency.
- Article 252 — When two or more States consent.
- Article 253 — Implementation of international agreements.
- Article 356 read with Article 357 — During President's Rule, as constitutionally provided.

### 8.3 Concurrent List

Both Parliament and State Legislatures may legislate on Concurrent List subjects.

Article 254: Where a State law conflicts with a valid Parliamentary law on a Concurrent List subject, the Parliamentary law ordinarily prevails.

A conflicting State law reserved for and receiving the President's assent may prevail within that State, subject to Parliament's power to subsequently override it.

### 8.4 Residuary powers

Under Article 248, Parliament has residuary legislative powers, including powers over subjects not enumerated in the State or Concurrent Lists.

### 8.5 Subjects transferred by the 42nd Amendment, 1976

Five subjects were transferred from the State List to the Concurrent List:

1. Education
2. Forests
3. Weights and measures
4. Protection of wild animals and birds
5. Administration of justice; constitution and organisation of courts other than the Supreme Court and High Courts

Important: GST has a special legislative arrangement under Article 246A, introduced by the 101st Amendment. It should not be treated simply as an ordinary Concurrent List subject.

## 9. Eighth Schedule — Languages

Relevant Articles: 344(1) and 351

The Eighth Schedule currently contains 22 languages.

### 9.1 All 22 languages

|          |          |           |          |
| -------- | -------- | --------- | -------- |
| Assamese | Bengali  | Bodo      | Dogri    |
| Gujarati | Hindi    | Kannada   | Kashmiri |
| Konkani  | Maithili | Malayalam | Manipuri |
| Marathi  | Nepali   | Odia      | Punjabi  |
| Sanskrit | Santhali | Sindhi    | Tamil    |
| Telugu   | Urdu     |           |          |

### 9.2 Amendments relating to languages

| Amendment | Year | Change                                   |
| --------- | ---- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 21st      | 1967 | Added Sindhi                             |
| 71st      | 1992 | Added Konkani, Manipuri and Nepali       |
| 92nd      | 2003 | Added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili and Santhali |
| 96th      | 2011 | Changed the name Oriya to Odia           |

Originally, the Eighth Schedule contained 14 languages.

### 9.3 Important distinctions

- The Constitution does not declare any language as the national language of India.
- Hindi in Devanagari script is the official language of the Union under Article 343.
- English continues to be used for Union official purposes under the applicable constitutional and statutory framework.
- Inclusion in the Eighth Schedule does not automatically make a language an official language of every State.
- States may adopt their official languages under Article 345.

Prelims trap: English is not one of the 22 languages listed in the Eighth Schedule.

## 10. Ninth Schedule — Protection of certain laws

Relevant Article: 31B

Introduced by: First Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951.

The Ninth Schedule was originally introduced primarily to protect land reform legislation from challenges based on Fundamental Rights.

### 10.1 Constitutional protection

Article 31B provides special protection to Acts and Regulations included in the Ninth Schedule against invalidation on the ground of inconsistency with Fundamental Rights, subject to the Basic Structure Doctrine.

### 10.2 Judicial review

In I. R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu (2007), the Supreme Court held that laws inserted into the Ninth Schedule after 24 April 1973 are subject to judicial review on basic-structure grounds.

Therefore:

- Ninth Schedule inclusion is not absolute immunity from judicial scrutiny.
- A law's inclusion after the Kesavananda Bharati judgment does not permit destruction of the Constitution's basic structure.

Prelims trap: The Ninth Schedule was not introduced by the 42nd Amendment, and its protection is not unlimited.

## 11. Tenth Schedule — Anti-Defection Law

Relevant Articles: 102(2) and 191(2)

Introduced by: 52nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1985.

The Tenth Schedule provides for disqualification of members of Parliament and State Legislatures on specified grounds of defection.

### 11.1 Grounds for disqualification

A legislator belonging to a political party may be disqualified for:

1. Voluntarily giving up membership of the political party.
2. Voting or abstaining contrary to a valid party direction without prior permission, where the act is not condoned within 15 days.

An independent member may be disqualified for joining a political party after election.

A nominated member may be disqualified for joining a political party after six months from taking the seat.

### 11.2 Merger exception

Disqualification does not apply to a qualifying merger under the Tenth Schedule where not less than two-thirds of the members of the legislature party agree to the merger.

The 91st Amendment Act, 2003 removed the earlier one-third split exception.

### 11.3 Who decides disqualification?

The Speaker or Chairman of the House ordinarily decides disqualification petitions under the Tenth Schedule.

Such decisions are subject to judicial review, as recognised in Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992).

Prelims traps

- Voluntarily giving up party membership need not always involve a formal written resignation.
- The 15-day period relates to condonation of a contrary vote or abstention, not a general grace period for defection.
- The two-thirds merger exception remains, but the one-third split exception does not.

## 12. Eleventh Schedule — Panchayats

Relevant Article: 243G

Introduced by: 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992.

Effective date: 24 April 1993.

The Eleventh Schedule contains 29 subjects relating to Panchayats.

### All 29 subjects

1. Agriculture, including agricultural extension.
2. Land improvement, implementation of land reforms, land consolidation and soil conservation.
3. Minor irrigation, water management and watershed development.
4. Animal husbandry, dairying and poultry.
5. Fisheries.
6. Social forestry and farm forestry.
7. Minor forest produce.
8. Small-scale industries, including food-processing industries.
9. Khadi, village and cottage industries.
10. Rural housing.
11. Drinking water.
12. Fuel and fodder.
13. Roads, culverts, bridges, ferries, waterways and other means of communication.
14. Rural electrification, including distribution of electricity.
15. Non-conventional energy sources.
16. Poverty alleviation programmes.
17. Education, including primary and secondary schools.
18. Technical training and vocational education.
19. Adult and non-formal education.
20. Libraries.
21. Cultural activities.
22. Markets and fairs.
23. Health and sanitation, including hospitals, primary health centres and dispensaries.
24. Family welfare.
25. Women and child development.
26. Social welfare, including welfare of persons with disabilities.
27. Welfare of weaker sections, particularly Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes.
28. Public distribution system.
29. Maintenance of community assets.

### Important constitutional distinction

The Eleventh Schedule identifies subjects that may be devolved to Panchayats.

It does not automatically transfer all 29 subjects to every Panchayat.

Under Article 243G, State Legislatures may endow Panchayats with powers and responsibilities by law.

## 13. Twelfth Schedule — Municipalities

Relevant Article: 243W

Introduced by: 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992.

Effective date: 1 June 1993.

The Twelfth Schedule contains 18 subjects relating to urban local government.

### All 18 subjects

1. Urban planning, including town planning.
2. Regulation of land use and construction of buildings.
3. Planning for economic and social development.
4. Roads and bridges.
5. Water supply for domestic, industrial and commercial purposes.
6. Public health, sanitation, conservancy and solid waste management.
7. Fire services.
8. Urban forestry, environmental protection and ecological promotion.
9. Safeguarding the interests of weaker sections of society, including persons with disabilities.
10. Slum improvement and upgradation.
11. Urban poverty alleviation.
12. Provision of urban amenities and facilities such as parks, gardens and playgrounds.
13. Promotion of cultural, educational and aesthetic aspects.
14. Burials and burial grounds; cremations, cremation grounds and electric crematoriums.
15. Cattle pounds and prevention of cruelty to animals.
16. Vital statistics, including registration of births and deaths.
17. Public amenities, including street lighting, parking lots, bus stops and public conveniences.
18. Regulation of slaughterhouses and tanneries.

As with Panchayats, inclusion in the Twelfth Schedule does not automatically confer every listed power on all Municipalities. State legislation determines the extent of devolution under Article 243W.

## 14. Eleventh versus Twelfth Schedule

| Feature             | Eleventh Schedule         | Twelfth Schedule          |

| Institution         | Panchayats                | Municipalities            |

## 16. High-priority UPSC Prelims revision

1. The Constitution originally had 8 Schedules and now has 12.
2. First Schedule — States and Union Territories.
3. Second Schedule — Emoluments and allowances of specified constitutional authorities.
4. Third Schedule — Oaths and affirmations; not the President's oath.
5. Fourth Schedule — Allocation of Rajya Sabha seats.
6. Fifth Schedule — Scheduled Areas and Scheduled Tribes outside the four Sixth Schedule States.
7. Sixth Schedule — Tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.
8. Seventh Schedule — Union, State and Concurrent Lists.
9. Eighth Schedule — 22 languages; no constitutionally declared national language.
10. Ninth Schedule — Article 31B protection, subject to basic-structure judicial review.
11. Tenth Schedule — Anti-Defection Law; two-thirds merger exception.
12. Eleventh Schedule — 29 subjects for Panchayats.
13. Twelfth Schedule — 18 subjects for Municipalities.
14. State Legislatures determine the extent of devolution of Eleventh and Twelfth Schedule subjects.
15. The Ninth, Tenth, Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules were added through constitutional amendments.

For UPSC Prelims, the most important areas are the distinction between the Fifth and Sixth Schedules, legislative powers under the Seventh Schedule, language amendments under the Eighth Schedule, Ninth Schedule judicial review, Tenth Schedule disqualification rules, and the 29-versus-18 subject distinction between the Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules.

## Quick reference

- Original Constitution: 8 Schedules; currently: 12.
- 1st Amendment: Ninth Schedule; 52nd: Tenth; 73rd: Eleventh; 74th: Twelfth.
- Eleventh Schedule: 29 subjects; Twelfth Schedule: 18 subjects.
- Fifth Schedule: Article 244(1); Sixth Schedule: Article 244(2).
- Seventh Schedule: Article 246; Eighth Schedule: 22 languages.
