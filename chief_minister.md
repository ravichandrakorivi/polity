# Chief Minister of a State

## 1. Constitutional position

The Chief Minister (CM) is the head of the elected State Government and the real executive authority under India's parliamentary system.

- Governor: Constitutional head of the State.
- Chief Minister: Head of the State Council of Ministers and the political executive.
- State Council of Ministers: Aids and advises the Governor in the exercise of constitutional functions, except where the Constitution permits discretionary action.
- Collective responsibility: The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the State Legislative Assembly, not the Governor or Legislative Council.

The Chief Minister at the State level broadly corresponds to the Prime Minister at the Union level.

## 2. Appointment of the Chief Minister

Article 164(1): The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor.

### 2.1 When a party has a clear majority

- The Governor ordinarily appoints the leader of the majority party or coalition in the Legislative Assembly as Chief Minister.
- The Constitution does not prescribe a separate direct election for the office of Chief Minister.
- The Chief Minister's authority to govern depends on retaining the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.

### 2.2 When no party has a clear majority

In a hung Legislative Assembly:

- The Governor exercises constitutional judgment to identify the person most likely to command majority support.
- The appointee may be the leader of a party or a coalition.
- The leader of the single largest party does not have an automatic right to become Chief Minister.
- Majority support should ordinarily be established through a floor test in the Legislative Assembly.

Example: If Party A wins 90 seats and Parties B and C together win 120 seats in a 230-member Assembly, Party A's leader does not automatically have the right to be appointed Chief Minister. The Governor must assess who is likely to command Assembly confidence.

## 3. Qualifications

The Constitution does not prescribe a separate list of qualifications specifically for the office of Chief Minister.

However, a Chief Minister must be eligible to become a member of the State Legislature.

| Requirement                                   | Position                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Citizenship                                   | Must be an Indian citizen |
| Minimum age for Legislative Assembly          | 25 years                  |
| Minimum age for Legislative Council           | 30 years                  |
| Legislative membership at appointment         | Not compulsory            |
| Maximum period without legislative membership | Six consecutive months    |

### Six-month rule — Article 164(4)

A person who is not a member of the State Legislature may be appointed Chief Minister.

However, that person must become a member of either House of the State Legislature within six consecutive months.

Otherwise, the person ceases to be a minister and consequently cannot continue as Chief Minister.

Important qualifications:

- In a unicameral State, the person must become a member of the Legislative Assembly.
- In a bicameral State, membership of either the Assembly or the Council is sufficient.
- The six-month rule cannot be repeatedly used to reappoint a non-legislator without obtaining legislative membership, as clarified in S. R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001).
- A person disqualified from being a legislator cannot circumvent that disqualification merely through the six-month provision, as clarified in B. R. Kapur v. State of Tamil Nadu (2001).

## 4. Oath and tenure

### 4.1 Oath

Under Article 164(3), the Governor administers the following oaths to the Chief Minister:

1. Oath of office.
2. Oath of secrecy.

The forms are prescribed in the Third Schedule of the Constitution.

### 4.2 Tenure

- The Constitution does not prescribe a fixed five-year term for the Chief Minister.
- The Chief Minister ordinarily remains in office as long as the government enjoys the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.
- Although Article 164 states that ministers hold office during the Governor's pleasure, this is governed by the principles of responsible parliamentary government.
- The Governor cannot arbitrarily dismiss a Chief Minister who continues to command Assembly confidence.

UPSC trap: The Legislative Assembly normally has a five-year term, but the Chief Minister does not have an independently guaranteed five-year term.

## 5. State Council of Ministers

### 5.1 Appointment of ministers

Under Article 164(1):

- The Governor appoints the Chief Minister.
- The Governor appoints other ministers on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- The Chief Minister recommends the composition of the ministry and allocation of portfolios.
- The Chief Minister may advise the Governor to remove or reshuffle ministers.

### 5.2 Collective responsibility — Article 164(2)

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.

This means:

- The ministry must retain the confidence of the Assembly.
- If the Assembly passes a no-confidence motion against the Council of Ministers, the entire ministry must resign.
- Ministers are expected to publicly support Cabinet decisions or resign.
- The Legislative Council cannot remove the ministry through a no-confidence vote.

### 5.3 Individual responsibility

Ministers hold office during the Governor's pleasure under Article 164(1).

In normal constitutional practice, the Governor acts on the Chief Minister's advice in matters concerning the continuation or removal of individual ministers.

### 5.4 Size of the Council of Ministers — 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003

Article 164(1A) provides:

- Total number of ministers, including the Chief Minister, must not exceed 15% of the total membership of the Legislative Assembly.
- A State must have at least 12 ministers, including the Chief Minister.

Example: If a State Legislative Assembly has 200 members, the Council of Ministers can have a maximum of 30 ministers, including the Chief Minister.

### 5.5 Disqualification on grounds of defection

Under Article 164(1B):

A member of the State Legislature disqualified under the Tenth Schedule is also disqualified from being appointed a minister for the period specified in Article 164(1B).

This restriction is connected to the 91st Constitutional Amendment.

## 6. Powers and functions of the Chief Minister

### 6.1 In relation to the Council of Ministers

The Chief Minister:

1. Recommends persons for appointment as ministers.
2. Allocates and reshuffles ministerial portfolios.
3. Presides over meetings of the State Cabinet.
4. Coordinates the functioning of different departments.
5. Guides major policies and decisions of the State Government.
6. May advise the Governor to remove a minister.
7. Acts as the principal link between the Governor and the Council of Ministers.

Important: If the Chief Minister resigns, the entire Council of Ministers ordinarily resigns because the ministry is headed by the Chief Minister.

### 6.2 In relation to the Governor — Article 167

Article 167 specifies three constitutional duties of the Chief Minister.

| Clause         | Duty                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Article 167(a) | Communicate to the Governor all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to State administration and legislative proposals                                 |
| Article 167(b) | Furnish information concerning State administration and legislative proposals whenever the Governor requests it                                                  |
| Article 167(c) | Submit for consideration of the Council of Ministers any matter decided by an individual minister but not considered by the Council, if the Governor so requires |

Example of Article 167(c): Suppose the Education Minister takes a significant policy decision without placing it before the Council of Ministers. The Governor may require the Chief Minister to place that matter before the Council for consideration.

This does not mean that the Governor personally makes the policy decision.

### 6.3 In relation to the State Legislature

The Chief Minister:

- Leads the government in the State Legislature.
- Plays a major role in determining the government's legislative agenda.
- Advises the Governor regarding summoning and proroguing legislative sessions, in accordance with constitutional requirements.
- May advise dissolution of the Legislative Assembly, subject to constitutional limitations.
- Defends government policies and legislative proposals.
- Is responsible for maintaining the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.

Important: A Chief Minister who belongs to the Legislative Council can head the State Government, provided the ministry commands the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.

### 6.4 Other important functions

The Chief Minister ordinarily:

- Heads the State's political executive.
- Coordinates policy implementation across departments.
- Plays a leading role in State development planning.
- Represents the State Government in intergovernmental discussions.
- Participates in the Governing Council of NITI Aayog.

## 7. Chief Minister and the State Legislature

### 7.1 Can a Legislative Council member become Chief Minister?

Yes.

Article 164 does not require the Chief Minister to belong specifically to the Legislative Assembly.

In a bicameral State, the Chief Minister may be a member of either:

- Legislative Assembly, or
- Legislative Council.

However, the Council of Ministers remains collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly.

### 7.2 Can the Chief Minister participate in both Houses?

Under Article 177, every State minister has the right to:

- Speak in either House of the State Legislature.
- Participate in proceedings of either House.
- Participate in legislative committees of which the minister is named a member.

However, Article 177 does not itself confer voting rights.

A Chief Minister who is a member of the Legislative Council cannot vote in the Legislative Assembly merely because of being Chief Minister.

## 8. Special provisions relating to State ministers

### 8.1 Minister for tribal welfare — Article 164(1)

The Constitution requires a minister in charge of tribal welfare in the following States:

- Chhattisgarh
- Jharkhand
- Madhya Pradesh
- Odisha

This minister may additionally be in charge of welfare of Scheduled Castes and backward classes or other work.

UPSC trap: The provision does not apply automatically to every State having a Scheduled Tribe population.

### 8.2 Salaries and allowances — Article 164(5)

Salaries and allowances of State ministers are determined by the State Legislature by law.

Until so determined, the provisions of the Second Schedule apply.

## 9. Chief Minister versus Governor

| Feature                   | Chief Minister                         | Governor                                                        |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Position                  | Head of elected State Government       | Constitutional head of State                                    |
| Appointment               | By Governor                            | By President                                                    |

## 12. Important UPSC Prelims traps

1. The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor, not directly elected by the people.
2. The leader of the single largest party does not have an unconditional right to appointment.
3. The Chief Minister need not be a legislator at the time of appointment, but must become one within six consecutive months.
4. A disqualified person cannot use the six-month rule to bypass constitutional disqualification.
5. A Chief Minister may belong to the Legislative Council, but the ministry is responsible to the Legislative Assembly.
6. The Chief Minister does not have a constitutionally guaranteed five-year term.
7. Other ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister.
8. The maximum ministry size is 15% of Assembly membership, including the Chief Minister.
9. The constitutional minimum is 12 ministers, including the Chief Minister.
10. Article 167 concerns the Chief Minister's duties to communicate decisions and furnish information to the Governor.
11. Article 177 gives ministers speaking and participation rights in both Houses, but not voting rights merely by virtue of ministerial office.
12. The Governor's formal executive powers do not mean that the Governor ordinarily governs independently of the elected ministry.

Core concept for Prelims: The Chief Minister heads the political executive of the State, but the Council of Ministers can remain in office only while it commands the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.

**Answer: C**
