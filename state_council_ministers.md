# State Council of Ministers

## 1. Constitutional position

India follows the parliamentary system of government at both the Union and State levels.

At the State level:

- Governor: Constitutional head of the State.
- Chief Minister: Head of the State Council of Ministers.
- State Council of Ministers: The body that exercises political executive authority and ordinarily aids and advises the Governor.

Under Article 163(1), there shall be a Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister to aid and advise the Governor, except where the Constitution requires the Governor to exercise functions in discretion.

The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the State Legislative Assembly, not to the Governor or Legislative Council.

## 3. Appointment and composition

### 3.1 Appointment of ministers — Article 164(1)

- The Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor.
- Other ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister.
- The Chief Minister recommends the persons to be appointed as ministers and ordinarily determines their portfolios.
- The Constitution does not require every minister to be a member of the Legislative Assembly specifically.

In a bicameral State, a minister may belong to either the Legislative Assembly or the Legislative Council.

### 3.2 Categories of ministers

In practice, State ministers are commonly classified into:

| Category           | Position                                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Cabinet Ministers  | Senior ministers who head important departments and participate in Cabinet decisions |
| Ministers of State | May hold independent charge of a department or assist a Cabinet Minister             |
| Deputy Ministers   | May assist senior ministers where such positions exist                               |

Important: The Constitution does not prescribe this three-tier classification of State ministers.

These categories arise from governmental practice.

## 4. Size of the Council of Ministers

### Article 164(1A) — 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003

The Constitution provides that:

1. The total number of ministers, including the Chief Minister, shall not exceed 15% of the total membership of the State Legislative Assembly.
2. The number of ministers, including the Chief Minister, shall not be less than 12.

The 15% limit is calculated using the membership of the Legislative Assembly, not the combined membership of the Assembly and Legislative Council.

### Example

Suppose a State Legislative Assembly has 200 members.

Maximum number of ministers:

$$
200\times\frac{15}{100}=30 
$$

Therefore, the Council of Ministers may contain a maximum of 30 ministers, including the Chief Minister.

Important exception: The Constitution contains transitional provisions relating to the commencement of the 91st Amendment. For Prelims, remember that the current general constitutional ceiling is 15%, subject to the minimum of 12.

## 5. Qualifications and legislative membership

### 5.1 Can a non-legislator become a minister?

Yes.

Under Article 164(4), a person who is not a member of the State Legislature may be appointed as a minister.

However, the person must become a member of the State Legislature within six consecutive months.

Otherwise, the person ceases to be a minister.

### 5.2 Legislative membership requirements

- In a unicameral State, the minister must become a member of the Legislative Assembly.
- In a bicameral State, membership of either the Legislative Assembly or Legislative Council is sufficient.
- The six-month provision does not permit a constitutionally disqualified person to bypass the disqualification.

### Important judgments

**B. R. Kapur v. State of Tamil Nadu (2001)**

A person disqualified from being a legislator cannot rely on Article 164(4) to become Chief Minister.

**S. R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001)**

A non-legislator cannot repeatedly be appointed as minister for successive six-month periods without becoming a member of the Legislature.

## 6. Oath, tenure and remuneration

### 6.1 Oath — Article 164(3)

Before entering office, every minister takes:

- Oath of office.
- Oath of secrecy.

The oaths are administered by the Governor.

The forms are prescribed in the Third Schedule of the Constitution.

### 6.2 Tenure

Article 164(1) provides that ministers hold office during the pleasure of the Governor.

However, in the parliamentary system:

- The Governor ordinarily acts on the advice of the Chief Minister regarding the continuation or removal of individual ministers.
- The Council of Ministers remains in office while it commands the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.
- Ministers do not have a constitutionally guaranteed five-year term.

### 6.3 Salaries and allowances — Article 164(5)

The salaries and allowances of State ministers are determined by the State Legislature by law.

Until so determined, the provisions of the Second Schedule apply.

## 7. Collective responsibility — Article 164(2)

The Constitution states:

> The Council of Ministers shall be collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly of the State.

### Meaning

Collective responsibility means:

1. The entire Council of Ministers is responsible to the Legislative Assembly for government policies and decisions.
2. Ministers are expected to support decisions taken collectively by the government.
3. The ministry must maintain the confidence of the Legislative Assembly.
4. If the Legislative Assembly passes a no-confidence motion against the ministry, the entire Council of Ministers must resign.

### Important distinctions

- Collective responsibility is to the Legislative Assembly, not the Legislative Council.
- The Legislative Council may discuss and criticise government policies but cannot remove the ministry through a no-confidence motion.
- The resignation of the Chief Minister ordinarily results in the resignation of the entire Council of Ministers.

UPSC trap: Even in a bicameral State, the Council of Ministers is not collectively responsible to both Houses.

## 8. Individual responsibility

Under Article 164(1), ministers hold office during the Governor's pleasure.

This is commonly described as individual responsibility.

In constitutional practice:

- The Chief Minister may advise the Governor to remove an individual minister.
- The Governor ordinarily acts on that advice.
- The removal of an individual minister does not automatically require the resignation of the entire Council of Ministers.

### Collective versus individual responsibility

| Feature              | Collective responsibility                               | Individual responsibility                                                   |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Constitutional basis | Article 164(2)                                          | Article 164(1)                                                              |
| Responsible to       | Legislative Assembly                                    | Formally, Governor                                                          |
| Political operation  | Entire ministry must retain Assembly confidence         | Individual ministers ordinarily continue with CM's support                  |
| Consequence          | Loss of Assembly confidence requires ministry to resign | An individual minister may be removed without dismissing the whole ministry |

## 9. Council of Ministers versus Cabinet

The Council of Ministers includes all categories of ministers.

The Cabinet is the smaller group of senior ministers that takes the principal policy decisions.

| Feature                    | Council of Ministers                                              | Cabinet                                                            |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Composition                | All ministers, including Cabinet Ministers and Ministers of State | Senior Cabinet Ministers                                           |
| Size                       | Larger                                                            | Smaller                                                            |
| Constitutional recognition | Expressly recognised under Articles 163–164                       | Not separately defined as a State-level constitutional institution |
| Function                   | Collectively responsible for State administration                 | Principal policy and decision-making body                          |
| Meetings                   | Full Council meets comparatively infrequently                     | Meets regularly for major decisions                                |
| Head                       | Chief Minister                                                    | Chief Minister                                                     |

Important distinction: Article 352(3) expressly defines the Union Cabinet for the purpose of a national emergency. It does not provide a corresponding constitutional definition of a State Cabinet.

## 10. Relationship with the Governor — Article 163

The State Council of Ministers ordinarily aids and advises the Governor.

### General rule

The Governor normally acts on ministerial aid and advice when exercising executive functions.

### Exception

The Governor may act in discretion where the Constitution permits or requires such discretion.

Examples of situations involving constitutionally limited judgment include:

- Appointment of a Chief Minister when no party or coalition has an established majority.
- Constitutional action relating to a loss of Assembly confidence.
- Certain matters concerning reservation of Bills for the President.

Important: The Governor does not possess a general power to disregard ministerial advice merely because the Governor disagrees with government policy.

## 11. Conduct of government business — Article 166

Article 166 provides:

1. All executive action of the State Government shall be expressed to be taken in the name of the Governor.
2. Orders and instruments made in the Governor's name shall be authenticated according to prescribed rules.
3. The Governor makes rules for the convenient transaction of State Government business and allocation of business among ministers, except where the Constitution requires discretionary action.

These rules ordinarily operate within the framework of responsible government and ministerial advice.

### Example

A government order issued by the Department of Education may formally be expressed in the name of the Governor.

This does not mean that the Governor personally took the policy decision.

## 12. Duties of the Chief Minister — Article 167

The Chief Minister has three important constitutional duties towards the Governor.

| Provision      | Duty                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Article 167(a) | Communicate decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to State administration and legislative proposals                                                       |
| Article 167(b) | Furnish information relating to State administration and legislative proposals as requested by the Governor                                                        |
| Article 167(c) | Submit a matter decided by an individual minister to the Council of Ministers for consideration, if the Governor so requires and the Council has not considered it |

### Example of Article 167(c)

Suppose a minister independently decides an important administrative matter without placing it before the Council of Ministers.

The Governor may require the Chief Minister to submit that matter to the Council for consideration.

The Governor does not thereby acquire the power to personally decide the policy.

## 13. Rights of ministers in the State Legislature — Article 177

Every State minister has the right to:

- Speak in the Legislative Assembly.
- Speak in the Legislative Council, where one exists.
- Take part in the proceedings of either House.
- Participate in proceedings of legislative committees of which the minister is named a member.

However, Article 177 does not confer voting rights merely because a person is a minister.

### Example

A minister who is a member of the Legislative Council may speak in the Legislative Assembly but cannot vote there solely by virtue of being a minister.

Voting rights depend on membership of the relevant House.

## 14. Disqualification on grounds of defection — Article 164(1B)

The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003 inserted Article 164(1B).

A member of the State Legislature who is disqualified under Paragraph 2 of the Tenth Schedule is also disqualified from being appointed as a minister.

The disqualification operates from the date of disqualification until:

- The date on which the member's original term would expire; or
- The date on which the person is elected to the Legislature again,

whichever is earlier.

Important: This provision is intended to discourage defections motivated by the prospect of ministerial office.

## 15. Special provision for tribal welfare ministers

Under Article 164(1), the Constitution requires a minister in charge of tribal welfare in:

1. Chhattisgarh
2. Jharkhand
3. Madhya Pradesh
4. Odisha

The minister may additionally be in charge of the welfare of Scheduled Castes and backward classes or other work.

This is a specific constitutional requirement for these four States.

## 16. State Council of Ministers versus Union Council of Ministers

| Feature                   | State Council of Ministers | Union Council of Ministers  |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| Head                      | Chief Minister             | Prime Minister              |
| Constitutional articles   | 163–164                    | 74–75                       |
| Appointing authority      | Governor                   | President                   |
| Collective responsibility | Legislative Assembly       | Lok Sabha                   |
| Maximum size              | 15% of Assembly membership | 15% of Lok Sabha membership |
| Minimum size              | 12 ministers               | No constitutional minimum   |
| Non-legislator rule       | Six months                 | Six months                  |
| Oath administered by      | Governor                   | President                   |
| Salary determined by      | State Legislature          | Parliament                  |

## 17. Important Supreme Court judgments

| Judgment                                  | Principle                                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)  | Governor ordinarily acts on ministerial aid and advice                                            |
| S. R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)     | Majority support should ordinarily be determined on the floor of the House                        |
| B. R. Kapur v. State of Tamil Nadu (2001) | Disqualified persons cannot circumvent disqualification through Article 164(4)                    |
| S. R. Chaudhuri v. State of Punjab (2001) | Repeated appointment of non-legislators without obtaining legislative membership is impermissible |
| Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016)      | Governor's discretionary powers are constitutionally limited                                      |

## 18. High-priority UPSC Prelims revision points

1. Article 163: Council of Ministers aids and advises the Governor, except in constitutionally discretionary matters.
2. Article 164(1): CM appointed by Governor; other ministers appointed on CM's advice.
3. Article 164(1A): Maximum 15% of Assembly membership; minimum 12 ministers.
4. Article 164(1B): Defection-related restriction on appointment as minister.
5. Article 164(2): Collective responsibility to Legislative Assembly alone.
6. Article 164(3): Oaths of office and secrecy administered by Governor.
7. Article 164(4): Non-legislator minister must become a member within six consecutive months.
8. Article 164(5): Ministerial salaries determined by State Legislature.
9. Article 166: Executive actions formally expressed in Governor's name.
10. Article 167: CM communicates decisions and furnishes information to Governor.
11. Article 177: Ministers can speak in both Houses but do not automatically possess voting rights in both.
12. Tribal welfare minister: Constitutionally required in Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh and Odisha.
13. Council of Ministers versus Cabinet: Council includes all ministers; Cabinet is the smaller senior decision-making body.
14. No-confidence motion: Only the Legislative Assembly can withdraw confidence from the ministry.
15. Governor's pleasure: Does not authorise arbitrary dismissal of a ministry enjoying Assembly confidence.

Most important conceptual distinction: The Governor is the constitutional head of the State, while the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers exercise political executive authority. The Council of Ministers must retain the confidence of the Legislative Assembly, even when the State has a Legislative Council.
