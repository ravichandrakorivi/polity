# Cabinet Committees in India

## 1. Meaning and constitutional position

Cabinet Committees are smaller groups of Union Ministers constituted to examine, coordinate and decide important matters relating to government policy and administration.

They help the Union Cabinet manage its extensive workload by allowing specialised examination of issues.

### Constitutional status

- Cabinet Committees are extra-constitutional bodies: they are not directly established by the Constitution.
- They are constituted under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961, framed under Article 77(3).
- Their composition and functions may be changed by the government.
- They are different from parliamentary committees, which are associated with the functioning of Parliament.

Important distinction: Article 352(3) recognises the Union Cabinet as the Council consisting of the Prime Minister and Cabinet-rank Ministers for the purpose of a written recommendation for a National Emergency. It does not establish Cabinet Committees.

## 2. Purpose and functions

Cabinet Committees are established to:

1. Examine important policy matters in greater detail.
2. Coordinate decisions involving several ministries.
3. Resolve differences between ministries.
4. Take decisions on matters assigned to them.
5. Recommend matters for consideration by the full Cabinet when required.
6. Improve administrative efficiency and speed up decision-making.

Cabinet Committees function within the collective responsibility framework of the Union Council of Ministers.

## 3. Formation, membership and chairmanship

### Formation

Cabinet Committees are constituted by the government, with their composition and allocation of business determined under the applicable executive rules.

They may be:

- Standing Cabinet Committees: Established for continuing areas of government business.
- Ad hoc Cabinet Committees: Constituted for particular matters and dissolved when their work is completed.

### Membership

- Members are ordinarily Union Cabinet Ministers.
- Ministers of State may also be included or invited as provided in the relevant composition and procedures.
- The Prime Minister is not necessarily a member of every Cabinet Committee.
- A Cabinet Committee need not consist exclusively of ministers belonging to a single ministry or department.

### Chairmanship

- The Prime Minister chairs several important Cabinet Committees.
- Other Cabinet Committees may be chaired by senior Union Ministers.
- The Prime Minister does not automatically chair every Cabinet Committee.

## 6. Functions of important Cabinet Committees

### 6.1 Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC)

The ACC considers and decides specified senior appointments and empanelments in the Union Government.

It deals with appointments covered by the relevant provisions of the Transaction of Business Rules.

Important: The ACC does not make every government appointment. Most routine appointments are handled through the prescribed departmental and recruitment procedures.

### 6.2 Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)

The CCEA deals with major economic matters, including:

- Economic policy issues requiring high-level decisions.
- Major investment and infrastructure proposals within its jurisdiction.
- Important economic proposals referred to it under the applicable rules.

### 6.3 Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS)

The CCS deals with major matters concerning:

- National security.
- Defence policy.
- Important security-related expenditure and procurement.
- Matters of strategic significance.

The CCS is a high-level executive decision-making body, not a constitutional defence council.

### 6.4 Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs

This committee considers major political and policy issues requiring coordination at the highest level of government.

It is traditionally regarded as one of the most influential Cabinet Committees.

### 6.5 Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs

This committee coordinates government-related parliamentary business, including legislative planning and parliamentary scheduling matters within its remit.

Important distinction: It is an executive committee and should not be confused with the Business Advisory Committee of either House of Parliament.

### 6.6 Cabinet Committee on Accommodation

It considers matters concerning government accommodation within its assigned jurisdiction.

### 6.7 Cabinet Committee on Investment and Growth

It deals with policy and administrative matters concerning investment promotion, investment facilitation and economic growth within its allocated business.

### 6.8 Cabinet Committee on Employment and Skill Development

It addresses major policy matters relating to employment opportunities, employability and skill development.

## 8. Role of the Cabinet Secretariat

The Cabinet Secretariat provides administrative and secretarial support to the Cabinet and Cabinet Committees.

Its responsibilities include:

- Arranging meetings on the orders of the Prime Minister.
- Preparing and circulating agendas.
- Circulating relevant Cabinet papers.
- Recording discussions and decisions.
- Communicating approved decisions.
- Facilitating coordination among ministries and departments.

The Cabinet Secretariat operates under the overall direction of the Prime Minister and is headed administratively by the Cabinet Secretary, who is a senior civil servant, not a Cabinet Minister.


## 9. Transaction of Business Rules versus Allocation of Business Rules

Both sets of rules are framed under Article 77(3), but they serve different purposes.

| Rules                                                     | Purpose                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961  | Distribute subjects and responsibilities among ministries and departments                                                     |
| Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961 | Prescribe how government business is processed and decided, including matters requiring Cabinet or Cabinet Committee approval |

## 10. Cabinet Committees and collective responsibility

Article 75(3) provides that the Union Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

Cabinet Committees operate within this system.

- They do not constitute separate governments.
- They cannot independently override constitutional provisions.
- Their decision-making authority depends on the business assigned to them.
- Their decisions form part of the executive decision-making framework.

Important: A Cabinet Committee may be authorised to decide specified matters. Therefore, it is incorrect to say that every Cabinet Committee can only make recommendations.

## 11. Historical developments

- Cabinet Committees have developed as instruments of executive coordination.

- Their number, names, composition and jurisdiction have changed under different governments.

- In 2019, the government constituted committees on Investment and Growth and Employment and Skill Development, alongside the existing major committees.

- The existence of a Cabinet Committee in a previous government does not guarantee that it remains constituted in the same form today.

## 12. UPSC Prelims traps

1. Cabinet Committees are extra-constitutional, not constitutional bodies.
2. They function under executive rules framed using Article 77(3).
3. They are different from parliamentary committees.
4. The Prime Minister does not chair every Cabinet Committee.
5. The ACC deals with specified senior appointments, not all public recruitment.
6. Cabinet Committees may take decisions within their delegated jurisdiction; they are not necessarily only recommendatory.
7. The Cabinet Committee on Parliamentary Affairs is not a parliamentary committee.
8. The Cabinet Committee on Security is an executive body, not a constitutional institution.
9. The Cabinet Secretary is a civil servant and does not become a Cabinet Minister by virtue of that office.
10. The number and composition of Cabinet Committees can change without a constitutional amendment.
11. The Cabinet Secretariat assists the Cabinet and its committees; it is not itself a Cabinet Committee.
