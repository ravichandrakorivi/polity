# Parliamentary Committees in India

## 1. Introduction

Parliamentary Committees are bodies constituted to examine legislative, financial, administrative and other matters in greater detail than is ordinarily possible during proceedings of the full Houses of Parliament.

They enable Parliament to exercise effective oversight over the executive and examine Bills, government expenditure and policies.

### 1.1 Characteristics of Parliamentary Committees

A Parliamentary Committee generally:

1. Is appointed or elected by a House of Parliament, or nominated by the Speaker of Lok Sabha or Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
2. Works under the direction of the relevant presiding officer.
3. Presents its report to the House or to the presiding officer.
4. Is serviced by the Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha Secretariat.

These are the conventional characteristics of parliamentary committees. Not every body containing Members of Parliament is necessarily a Parliamentary Committee.

### 1.2 Constitutional and procedural basis

- The Constitution does not prescribe the detailed structure of most Parliamentary Committees.
- Their composition, functions and procedures are mainly governed by the Rules of Procedure of the two Houses and directions issued by their presiding officers.
- Certain committees have specific constitutional recognition. For example, Article 344(4) provides for a parliamentary committee concerning the Official Language.
- Parliamentary committees derive certain powers and privileges from Article 105.

UPSC distinction: Parliamentary Committees are part of the legislature, whereas Cabinet Committees are part of the executive.

## 2. Classification of Parliamentary Committees

Parliamentary Committees are broadly classified into two categories:

1. **Standing Committees**
2. **Ad Hoc Committees**

### 2.1 Standing Committees

Standing Committees are **permanent in nature**, although their members are appointed, elected or nominated periodically.

They deal with recurring matters relating to parliamentary functioning, financial oversight, legislation and government administration.

**Major categories:**

1. **Financial Committees**
   - Public Accounts Committee (PAC)
   - Estimates Committee
   - Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)

2. **Department-related Standing Committees (DRSCs)**
   - Examine the work, expenditure and policies of Union Ministries and Departments.
   - There are 24 DRSCs.

3. **Other Standing Committees**
   - Committee on Petitions
   - Committee of Privileges
   - Committee on Government Assurances
   - Committee on Subordinate Legislation
   - Business Advisory Committee
   - Rules Committee
   - Other committees concerned with parliamentary business and members' welfare.

### 2.2 Ad Hoc Committees

Ad Hoc Committees are **temporary committees constituted for specific purposes**.

They generally cease to exist after completing their assigned work and submitting their reports.

**Major categories:**

1. **Committees on Bills**
   - Select Committee on a Bill — consists of members of one House.
   - Joint Committee on a Bill — consists of members from both Houses.

2. **Inquiry Committees**
   - Constituted to investigate or examine specific matters.
   - Example: A Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC) constituted for a particular inquiry.

### 2.3 Standing Committees vs Ad Hoc Committees

| Feature | Standing Committees | Ad Hoc Committees |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Permanent institutions | Temporary, task-specific bodies |
| Tenure | Members reconstituted periodically | Usually exist until assigned work is completed |
| Functions | Recurring parliamentary responsibilities | Specific Bills, inquiries or assigned tasks |
| Examples | PAC, Estimates Committee, DRSCs | Select Committee, Joint Committee on a Bill, inquiry JPC |

## 3. Financial Committees

The three principal Financial Committees are:

1. Public Accounts Committee (PAC)
2. Estimates Committee
3. Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)

### 3.1 Comparative overview

| Feature                  | PAC                            | Estimates Committee          | COPU                |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Total members            | 22                             | 30                           | 22                  |
| Lok Sabha members        | 15                             | 30                           | 15                  |
| Rajya Sabha members      | 7                              | 0                            | 7                   |
| Tenure                   | 1 year                         | 1 year                       | 1 year              |
| Ministers eligible       | No                             | No                           | No                  |
| Chairperson appointed by | Lok Sabha Speaker              | Lok Sabha Speaker            | Lok Sabha Speaker   |
| Main focus               | Expenditure and accountability | Budget estimates and economy | Public undertakings |

Members are elected annually by the respective Houses through proportional representation by means of the single transferable vote.

### 3.2 Public Accounts Committee (PAC)

Historical background

- First established in 1921 under the Government of India Act, 1919 framework.
- Reconstituted under the parliamentary system after Independence.

Composition

- 22 members: 15 from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha.
- Term: One year.
- Ministers cannot be members.
- Members elected annually through proportional representation by STV.
- Chairperson appointed by the Lok Sabha Speaker from among committee members.
- By established convention, the chairperson is drawn from the Opposition, although this is not a constitutional requirement.

Functions

- Examines the Appropriation Accounts of the Union Government.
- Examines the Finance Accounts and reports of the Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG).
- Checks whether expenditure was incurred for the purposes authorised by Parliament.
- Examines whether expenditure complied with applicable rules and financial authority.
- Identifies irregularities, wasteful expenditure and weaknesses in financial administration.

Limitations

- Primarily examines expenditure after it has been incurred.
- Does not ordinarily question the merits of government policy as such.
- Cannot itself impose punishments or recover public money.

Important distinction: The CAG audits government accounts and submits reports; the PAC examines relevant CAG reports and seeks accountability from the executive.

### 3.3 Estimates Committee

Historical background

The first Estimates Committee in independent India was constituted in 1950.

Composition

- 30 members, all from Lok Sabha.
- Rajya Sabha has no representation.
- Members elected annually through proportional representation by STV.
- Chairperson appointed by the Lok Sabha Speaker.
- Ministers cannot be members.

Functions

- Examines Budget estimates.
- Suggests economies and improvements in government expenditure.
- Recommends administrative efficiency and organisational improvements.
- Suggests alternative policies to improve efficiency and economy within the framework of existing policy.
- Examines whether money is being used efficiently.

Important distinction: The Estimates Committee does not vote on Demands for Grants. Voting on Demands for Grants is the function of Lok Sabha.

### 3.4 Committee on Public Undertakings (COPU)

Historical background

Established in 1964 on the recommendation of the Krishna Menon Committee.

Composition

- 22 members: 15 from Lok Sabha and 7 from Rajya Sabha.
- Term: One year.
- Members elected annually through proportional representation by STV.
- Chairperson appointed by the Lok Sabha Speaker.
- Ministers cannot be members.

Functions

- Examines reports and accounts of specified public undertakings.
- Examines CAG reports concerning public undertakings.
- Examines whether public undertakings are managed according to sound business principles and prudent commercial practices.
- Examines efficiency and autonomy in the management of public undertakings.

Limitations

- Does not ordinarily examine day-to-day administration.
- Does not investigate matters of major government policy as distinct from business or commercial functions.
- Its jurisdiction is limited to undertakings assigned under the applicable parliamentary rules.

## 4. Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committees (DRSCs)

### 4.1 Background and composition

- Established in 1993, initially with 17 committees.
- Expanded to 24 committees in 2004.
- Cover the Ministries and Departments of the Union Government.
- Each committee has a maximum of 31 members: 21 from Lok Sabha and 10 from Rajya Sabha.
- Members are nominated by the Speaker of Lok Sabha and Chairman of Rajya Sabha, respectively.
- Tenure does not exceed one year.
- Ministers cannot be members.

### 4.2 Functions

DRSCs examine:

1. Demands for Grants of the Ministries and Departments concerned.
2. Bills referred to them by the Speaker or Chairman.
3. Annual reports of the Ministries and Departments.
4. Long-term policy documents referred to them.

Important limitations:

- They do not vote on Demands for Grants.
- They do not ordinarily consider matters of day-to-day administration.
- They do not generally consider matters assigned to other parliamentary committees.
- Their reports and recommendations are advisory, not binding.

### 4.3 Administrative control

| Under Lok Sabha Speaker | Under Rajya Sabha Chairman |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- |
| 16 committees           | 8 committees               |

The eight committees under Rajya Sabha are concerned with:

- Commerce
- Home Affairs
- Education, Women, Children, Youth and Sports
- Industry
- Science and Technology, Environment, Forests and Climate Change
- Transport, Tourism and Culture
- Health and Family Welfare
- Personnel, Public Grievances, Law and Justice

The remaining 16 function under Lok Sabha.

UPSC trap: DRSCs include members from both Houses even though an individual committee may function under the administrative control of only one presiding officer.

## 5. Other Important Standing Committees

### 5.1 Committees to inquire

| Committee               | Main function                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Committee on Petitions  | Examines petitions concerning Bills and matters of public interest within its jurisdiction |
| Committee of Privileges | Examines matters involving alleged breaches of parliamentary privilege                     |
| Committee on Ethics     | Examines matters concerning the ethical conduct of MPs                                     |

Privileges versus Ethics:

- Privileges concern the special rights and immunities necessary for parliamentary functioning.
- Ethics concerns standards of conduct and propriety expected of MPs.

### 5.2 Committees to scrutinise and control

| Committee                             | Main function                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Committee on Government Assurances    | Monitors assurances, promises and undertakings made by ministers in Parliament                     |
| Committee on Subordinate Legislation  | Examines rules, regulations, orders and bye-laws made under delegated legislative authority        |
| Committee on Papers Laid on the Table | Scrutinises papers and documents laid before the House for compliance with applicable requirements |

Example: If Parliament enacts a law authorising the government to make rules, the Committee on Subordinate Legislation may examine whether those rules remain within the authority delegated by Parliament.

### 5.3 Committees relating to parliamentary business

| Committee                                                  | Main function                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Business Advisory Committee                                | Recommends allocation of time for parliamentary business                                  |
| Rules Committee                                            | Examines and recommends changes to the Rules of Procedure                                 |
| Committee on Private Members' Bills and Resolutions        | Examines classification and allocation of time for private members' business in Lok Sabha |
| Committee on Absence of Members from Sittings of the House | Examines applications for leave of absence from Lok Sabha sittings                        |

The last two committees are specific to Lok Sabha in this form.

### 5.4 Committees concerned with facilities and welfare

| Committee                           | Main function                                                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| General Purposes Committee          | Advises on matters concerning the affairs of the House                  |
| House Committee                     | Deals with residential accommodation and related facilities for members |
| Library Committee                   | Advises on parliamentary library facilities and services                |
| Committee on Welfare of SCs and STs | Examines matters relating to the welfare and safeguards of SCs and STs  |
| Committee on Empowerment of Women   | Examines measures relating to women's welfare, status and empowerment   |

These classifications follow the parliamentary committee framework published by the Rajya Sabha and Lok Sabha Secretariats.

## 6. Ad Hoc Parliamentary Committees

Ad Hoc Committees are constituted for a specific purpose and generally cease to exist after completing their assigned work.

### 6.1 Select Committee on a Bill

- Consists of members of one House.
- Examines a Bill referred to it.
- May examine individual clauses, receive evidence and suggest amendments.
- Submits its report to the House concerned.

### 6.2 Joint Committee on a Bill

- Consists of members from both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha.
- Examines a Bill referred to it by Parliament.
- May recommend changes to the Bill.
- Its recommendations are not automatically binding on Parliament.

### 6.3 Joint Parliamentary Committee (JPC)

A JPC may be constituted for a specific inquiry or investigation through the appropriate parliamentary process.

Its composition, mandate and powers depend on the terms under which it is constituted.

Examples of historical JPC inquiries include those concerning securities-market irregularities and the allocation of telecom licences.

### 6.4 Select Committee versus Joint Committee versus JPC

| Feature                  | Select Committee             | Joint Committee on Bill      | Inquiry JPC      |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------- |
| Membership               | One House                    | Both Houses                  | Both Houses      |
| Primary purpose          | Detailed examination of Bill | Detailed examination of Bill | Specific inquiry |
| Duration                 | Task-specific                | Task-specific                | Task-specific    |
| Recommendations binding? | No                           | No                           | No               |

UPSC trap: A Joint Committee on a Bill is not the same as a joint sitting under Article 108. A joint sitting is a constitutional mechanism for resolving certain legislative deadlocks.

## 7. Parliamentary Committee Procedure and Powers

### 7.1 Examination of evidence

Parliamentary Committees may, within their authorised powers:

- Seek information and records.
- Invite experts and stakeholders.
- Examine government officials and other witnesses.
- Conduct studies and visits.
- Prepare reports containing findings and recommendations.

### 7.2 Committee reports

- Reports are ordinarily presented to the relevant House or Houses.
- Recommendations are generally advisory, not legally binding.
- The government may be required under applicable procedures to submit action-taken responses.
- Committees may review the implementation of their recommendations.

### 7.3 Confidentiality

Committee proceedings are generally not open to the public unless permitted under applicable rules.

Evidence and reports may be subject to confidentiality restrictions until authorised publication or presentation.

### 7.4 Relationship with parliamentary accountability

Parliamentary Committees supplement, rather than replace, the authority of the Houses.

They cannot ordinarily enact laws, pass the Budget or remove ministers by themselves.

## 11. High-Priority Prelims Traps

1. The Estimates Committee is the only one of the three principal Financial Committees consisting exclusively of Lok Sabha members.
2. The PAC examines CAG reports but does not itself conduct the constitutional audit performed by the CAG.
3. DRSC members are nominated, whereas members of the three principal Financial Committees are elected.
4. Parliamentary Committee recommendations are generally advisory.
5. Not all Parliamentary Committees are constituted jointly by both Houses.
6. A committee report on a Bill does not constitute parliamentary passage of the Bill.
7. The Committee on Subordinate Legislation examines delegated legislation, not the internal Rules of Procedure of Parliament.
8. A Standing Committee is permanent in nature, but its membership is periodically reconstituted.
9. A Joint Parliamentary Committee is not a joint sitting of Parliament.
10. The Public Accounts Committee's Opposition chairperson is a convention, not a constitutional requirement.

> **Explanation:** Joint Committees scrutinise Bills and recommend changes. A joint sitting under Article 108 is a distinct constitutional mechanism for resolving certain deadlocks between the Houses. It is unavailable for constitutional amendment Bills.
