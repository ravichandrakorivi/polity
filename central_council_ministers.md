# Central Council of Ministers

## 1. Constitutional Position

- India follows a **parliamentary system of government**.
- The **President is the nominal executive**.
- The **Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister, exercises effective executive authority**.
- The Council aids and advises the President and is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha**.

| Article | Main provision |
|---|---|
| **74** | Council of Ministers to aid and advise the President |
| **75** | Appointment, size, tenure, responsibility, oaths, membership and remuneration of ministers |
| **77** | Conduct of the business of the Government of India |
| **78** | Prime Minister’s duties concerning communication and information to the President |
| **88** | Ministers’ rights to participate in parliamentary proceedings |
| **352(3)** | Written Cabinet decision required for a National Emergency proclamation |

---

## 2. Appointment and Parliamentary Membership

### Appointment

- The **President appoints the Prime Minister**.
- Other ministers are appointed by the **President on the advice of the Prime Minister**.
- The Prime Minister determines the distribution and reshuffling of ministerial portfolios.

### Parliamentary membership

- A Union minister may belong to **either House of Parliament**.
- An otherwise eligible person who is not a member of either House may also be appointed.
- Under **Article 75(5)**, a minister who remains outside both Houses for **six consecutive months** ceases to be a minister at the end of that period.

> Membership of either the **Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha** satisfies the requirement. Entry into the Lok Sabha specifically is not compulsory.

---

## 3. Size of the Council: 91st Amendment

Under **Article 75(1A)**:

> The total number of Union ministers, including the Prime Minister, cannot exceed **15% of the total number of members of the Lok Sabha**.

- Introduced by the **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003**.
- Includes **all categories of ministers**, not merely Cabinet Ministers.
- Rajya Sabha membership is **not included** when calculating the ceiling.
- Article 75 does not prescribe a minimum number of Union ministers.

**Example:** For a Lok Sabha of 543 members:

- 15% of 543 = 81.45.
- Maximum permissible number of ministers = **81**, including the Prime Minister.

> Figures such as “60–70 ministers” or “15–20 Cabinet Ministers” are illustrative, not fixed constitutional requirements.

---

## 4. Defection and Ministerial Appointment

Under **Article 75(1B)**, a member of Parliament disqualified for defection under **paragraph 2 of the Tenth Schedule** is also barred from appointment as a minister.

The bar continues until the earlier of:

1. The date on which the original parliamentary term would have expired.
2. The date on which the person is **declared elected** in an election contested before that expiry.

---

- This provision was also introduced by the **91st Amendment** 
- Merely contesting an election does **not** remove the bar.
- The six-month rule for non-MPs cannot be used to bypass this disqualification.

---

## 5. Oaths, Tenure and Remuneration

| Aspect | Provision |
|---|---|
| **Oaths** | Office and secrecy |
| **Administered by** | President |
| **Forms of oaths** | Third Schedule |
| **Tenure** | During the pleasure of the President |
| **Fixed five-year term?** | No |
| **Salaries and allowances** | Determined by Parliament by law |

### Oath of office

A minister undertakes to:

- Bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution.
- Uphold India’s sovereignty and integrity.
- Discharge duties faithfully and conscientiously.
- Act according to the Constitution and law without fear or favour, affection or ill will.

### Oath of secrecy

- A minister must protect matters acquired through ministerial office.
- Disclosure is permitted when required for the **due discharge of ministerial duties**.

---

## 6. Responsibility of Ministers

### A. Collective responsibility

**Article 75(3): The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.**

This means:

- Ministers function as a team and share responsibility for government decisions.
- Collective decisions bind **all ministers**, including Ministers of State.
- Ministers may disagree during internal deliberations.
- Once a decision is adopted, a minister must **support it publicly or resign** if unwilling to do so.
- If the Lok Sabha passes a no-confidence motion, the ministry must resign.
- This includes ministers belonging to the **Rajya Sabha**.

> Collective responsibility is to the **Lok Sabha**, not to both Houses separately.

### B. Individual responsibility

**Article 75(2): Ministers hold office during the pleasure of the President.**

- The Prime Minister may ask an individual minister to resign.
- The Prime Minister may advise the President to remove that minister.
- Such removal is possible even when the ministry retains the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
- The President acts on the Prime Minister’s advice in removing an individual minister.

### Quick comparison

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Collective responsibility** | The entire ministry is accountable to the Lok Sabha |
| **Individual responsibility** | An individual minister may be removed by the President on the Prime Minister’s advice |

---

## 7. Relationship with the President

### Binding advice: Article 74

- The Council of Ministers aids and advises the President.
- The President acts in accordance with that advice.
- The President may require the Council to **reconsider** its advice.
- The Council may modify or reiterate its advice.
- Advice tendered **after reconsideration is binding**.

| Amendment | Change |
|---|---|
| **42nd Amendment, 1976** | Expressly made ministerial advice binding |
| **44th Amendment, 1978** | Allowed presidential reconsideration; advice after reconsideration remains binding |

### Judicial inquiry: Article 74(2)

- Courts cannot inquire into **whether ministerial advice was tendered or what that advice was**.
- This protection does not make the resulting executive action immune from judicial review.
- The underlying material supporting an executive action is distinguishable from the ministerial advice itself.

### Continuity after Lok Sabha dissolution

- Dissolution of the Lok Sabha does **not automatically terminate the Council of Ministers**.
- The constitutional requirement for ministerial aid and advice continues.
- The President cannot use dissolution as a basis for governing without a Council of Ministers.

---

## 8. Conduct of Government Business: Article 77

Article 77 provides that:

1. Union executive action is expressed to be taken **in the President’s name**.
2. Orders and instruments are authenticated in the prescribed manner.
3. The President makes rules for:
   - Convenient transaction of government business.
   - Allocation of business among ministers.

### Effect of authentication

A properly authenticated order cannot be challenged merely because it was not personally made or executed by the President.

However:

- The President need not personally sign every executive order.
- Authentication does not protect an order from challenge for illegality or constitutional violation.

---

## 9. Prime Minister’s Communication Duties: Article 78

The Prime Minister must:

1. **Communicate** Council decisions concerning Union administration and legislative proposals to the President.
2. **Furnish information** on these matters when the President requests it.
3. **Submit a minister’s decision to the Council**, if the President requires, when the Council has not already considered the matter.

> The Prime Minister is the principal constitutional channel of communication between the President and the Council of Ministers.

---

## 10. Ministers’ Parliamentary Rights: Article 88

A minister may speak and participate in:

- Either House of Parliament.
- A joint sitting of the Houses.
- A parliamentary committee **of which the minister is named a member**, subject to applicable rules.

**Ministerial office alone does not confer voting rights.**

| Minister’s membership | Participation in either House | Voting in a House |
|---|---|---|
| Lok Sabha member | Permitted | In Lok Sabha |
| Rajya Sabha member | Permitted | In Rajya Sabha |
| Member of neither House | Permitted as a minister | In neither House |

> A minister who is an MP may vote at a joint sitting by virtue of parliamentary membership. Article 88 itself does not grant voting rights.

---

## 11. Categories of Ministers

| Category | Main role | Cabinet membership |
|---|---|---|
| **Cabinet Minister** | Heads an important ministry and participates in major collective policy decisions | Yes |
| **Minister of State with independent charge** | Administers an assigned ministry or department without a Cabinet Minister supervising that charge | No automatic membership; may attend by invitation |
| **Minister of State attached to a Cabinet Minister** | Handles assigned responsibilities under the Cabinet Minister’s overall charge | No |
| **Deputy Minister, where appointed** | Assists senior ministers in administrative, political and parliamentary work; ordinarily has no independent charge | No |

### Important distinctions

- **Independent charge does not mean Cabinet rank.**
- Invitation to a Cabinet meeting does **not** make a Minister of State a Cabinet member.
- Every government need not appoint Deputy Ministers.
- The Constitution does not prescribe a compulsory three-tier ministerial structure.

---

## 12. Council of Ministers versus Cabinet

| Basis | Council of Ministers | Cabinet |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition** | Entire ministry, including all ministerial ranks | Prime Minister and ministers of Cabinet rank |
| **Size** | Wider body; subject to the 15% ceiling | Smaller body within the Council |
| **Constitutional reference** | Articles 74 and 75 | Expressly defined in Article 352(3) |
| **Working** | The full body does not ordinarily conduct routine collective policy deliberations | Meets regularly for major collective decisions |
| **Role** | Wider ministerial machinery responsible for government administration | Principal body for collective policy formulation and coordination |
| **Responsibility** | Entire Council collectively responsible to Lok Sabha | Cabinet decisions bind the wider ministry |

> **Every Cabinet Minister belongs to the Council of Ministers, but every Council member is not a Cabinet Minister.**

### Major functions of the Cabinet

- Formulates major government policies.
- Coordinates ministries and resolves interdepartmental differences.
- Directs the government’s legislative and financial programme.
- Handles major foreign policy, security and crisis-management decisions.
- Supervises implementation of collective decisions.

---

## 13. Cabinet and National Emergency

The **44th Amendment** inserted express recognition of the Union Cabinet in **Article 352**.

Under **Article 352(3)**:

- The President cannot proclaim a National Emergency unless the **Union Cabinet’s decision is communicated in writing**.
- For this purpose, the Cabinet consists of the **Prime Minister and other ministers of Cabinet rank appointed under Article 75**.

### Prelims distinctions

- The Prime Minister’s personal recommendation is **insufficient**.
- An oral Cabinet recommendation is **insufficient**.
- The Constitution requires a Cabinet decision, not a unanimous resolution of every member of the wider Council of Ministers.

---

## 14. Deputy Prime Minister

- “Deputy Prime Minister” is a **political designation**, not a separate constitutional office.
- Its holder is appointed as a minister under Article 75.
- The designation does not itself confer the Prime Minister’s constitutional powers.
- It does not create an automatic right to succeed the Prime Minister.
- Actual responsibilities depend on assigned portfolios and functions.

---

## 15. Kitchen Cabinet

A **Kitchen Cabinet**, or inner circle, is an informal group of trusted advisers around the Prime Minister.

### Features

- It is not created by the Constitution.
- It may include ministers and persons outside the ministry.
- Its membership and influence vary.
- It can assist with confidential discussion and quick consultation.

### Main concern

- Informal advisers may influence decisions without the parliamentary accountability attached to ministerial office.
- Excessive reliance on this group may weaken formal Cabinet deliberation.

> A Kitchen Cabinet cannot replace the Union Cabinet where the Constitution requires a formal Cabinet decision.

---

## 16. Quick Revision: Common Prelims Traps

| Incorrect statement | Correct position |
|---|---|
| Ministers must belong to the Lok Sabha | They may belong to either House |
| A non-MP can never become a minister | An otherwise eligible non-MP may serve subject to the six-month rule |
| The 15% ceiling applies only to Cabinet Ministers | It applies to all Union ministers, including the Prime Minister |
| The ceiling uses the combined strength of Parliament | It uses Lok Sabha membership |
| Ministers have a guaranteed five-year term | No fixed constitutional tenure |
| Collective responsibility is to both Houses | It is to the Lok Sabha |
| Rajya Sabha ministers are exempt from a Lok Sabha no-confidence outcome | The entire ministry is affected |
| The President may disregard advice after reconsideration | Reconsidered advice is binding |
| Dissolution of Lok Sabha automatically ends the ministry | Ministerial aid and advice must continue |
| Article 88 allows ministers to vote in either House | Participation rights do not themselves confer voting rights |
| Independent charge automatically confers Cabinet membership | It does not |
| Deputy Prime Minister automatically succeeds the Prime Minister | No automatic constitutional succession |
| The Cabinet has a constitutionally fixed size of twenty | No such fixed size is prescribed |

> **Explanation:** Deputy Ministers, where appointed, assist senior ministers and ordinarily do not hold independent charge. The Constitution does not require every government to appoint ministers in this category.
