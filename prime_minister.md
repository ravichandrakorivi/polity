# Prime Minister of India

## 1. Constitutional Position

- The **President is the head of the State** and the nominal executive.
- The **Prime Minister is the head of the government** and leads the effective political executive.
- The executive power of the Union is formally vested in the President.
- The Council of Ministers, headed by the Prime Minister, aids and advises the President.

| Article | Main provision |
|---|---|
| **74** | Council of Ministers, with the Prime Minister at its head, to aid and advise the President |
| **75** | Appointment, tenure, collective responsibility, oaths, parliamentary membership and salaries of ministers |
| **77** | Conduct of the business of the Government of India |
| **78** | Duties of the Prime Minister concerning communication and information to the President |

---

## 2. Appointment

The **President appoints the Prime Minister under Article 75(1)**.

### When a party has a clear majority

- The President appoints the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha.
- The President’s choice is governed by parliamentary convention.

### When no party has a clear majority

- The President exercises judgment in identifying the person most likely to command the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
- The appointee may be the leader of a coalition.
- The leader of the single largest party does **not** have an unconditional right to appointment.
- Majority support can be demonstrated through a **floor test in the Lok Sabha**.

> **Prelims distinction:** A person need not prove a majority before being appointed. Appointment may precede a floor test. The Constitution does not prescribe a universal one-month deadline for proving a majority.

### When the incumbent Prime Minister dies

- There is **no automatic constitutional succession** by the senior-most minister.
- The President appoints the successor.
- If the party commanding a majority chooses a new leader, parliamentary convention guides the President to appoint that leader.

---

## 3. Parliamentary Membership

The Prime Minister may be:

- A member of the **Lok Sabha**.
- A member of the **Rajya Sabha**.
- An otherwise eligible person who is initially **not a member of either House**.

Under **Article 75(5)**:

> A minister who remains outside both Houses of Parliament for six consecutive months ceases to be a minister at the end of that period.

Therefore, a Prime Minister appointed without parliamentary membership must become a member of **either House within six months** to continue in office.

### Participation and voting

| Position of the Prime Minister | Participation | Voting |
|---|---|---|
| Lok Sabha member | May speak and participate in either House | May vote in the Lok Sabha |
| Rajya Sabha member | May speak and participate in either House | May vote in the Rajya Sabha |
| Initially a member of neither House | May speak and participate in either House as a minister | Cannot vote in either House |

**Article 88** grants ministers participation rights in either House. It does not grant voting rights merely because they hold ministerial office.

> A Rajya Sabha member serving as Prime Minister must still head a ministry that enjoys the confidence of the **Lok Sabha**.

---

## 4. Oaths, Tenure and Salary

### Oaths

- The **President** administers the oaths of **office and secrecy**.
- Their forms are provided in the **Third Schedule**.
- The Prime Minister takes the same forms of oath prescribed for Union ministers.

The oath of office includes commitments to:

- Bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution.
- Uphold India’s sovereignty and integrity.
- Discharge duties faithfully and conscientiously.
- Act according to the Constitution and law, without fear or favour, affection or ill will.

The oath of secrecy permits disclosure when required for the **due discharge of ministerial duties**.

### Tenure

- The Prime Minister has **no fixed constitutional term**.
- Ministers formally hold office during the **pleasure of the President**.
- This operates within the parliamentary requirement of maintaining the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
- The President cannot arbitrarily dismiss a Prime Minister who retains that confidence.
- A Prime Minister who loses the confidence of the Lok Sabha cannot insist on continuing in office.

> The five-year normal term of the Lok Sabha is **not a guaranteed five-year tenure for the Prime Minister**.

### Salary

- Parliament determines ministerial salaries and allowances by law under **Article 75(6)**.

---

## 5. Powers Relating to the Council of Ministers

The Prime Minister:

1. **Recommends appointments:** Other ministers are appointed by the President on the Prime Minister’s advice.
2. **Distributes portfolios:** Allocates and reshuffles responsibilities among ministers.
3. **Coordinates policy:** Guides and coordinates the work of ministries.
4. **Leads Cabinet deliberations:** Chairs Cabinet meetings and influences government decisions.
5. **Secures ministerial removal:** May ask a minister to resign or advise the President to remove the minister.
6. **Heads the ministry as a whole:** The Prime Minister’s resignation entails the resignation of the entire Council of Ministers.

### Prime Minister’s resignation versus another minister’s resignation

| Event | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Prime Minister resigns | The ministry as a whole resigns |
| An individual minister resigns | Ordinarily creates a vacancy; the ministry can continue |
| Prime Minister dies | A successor must be appointed and the ministry reconstituted |

A resigning ministry may be requested to continue in a **caretaker capacity** until alternative arrangements are made.

### Limit on the ministry’s size

Under **Article 75(1A)**:

- The total number of Union ministers, **including the Prime Minister**, cannot exceed **15% of the total number of Lok Sabha members**.
- This provision was introduced by the **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003**.

---

## 6. Relationship with the President

### A. Aid and advice: Article 74

- The Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister aids and advises the President.
- The President normally acts in accordance with this advice.
- The President may require the Council to **reconsider** its advice.
- The advice tendered **after reconsideration is binding**.

| Amendment | Change |
|---|---|
| **42nd Amendment, 1976** | Expressly required the President to act in accordance with ministerial advice |
| **44th Amendment, 1978** | Enabled the President to require reconsideration; advice after reconsideration remains binding |

### B. Communication duties: Article 78

The Prime Minister must:

1. **Communicate decisions** of the Council concerning Union administration and proposals for legislation.
2. **Furnish information** on these matters when the President calls for it.
3. **Place a minister’s decision before the Council**, if the President requires, when that matter has not already been considered by the Council.

> The Prime Minister is the principal constitutional channel of communication between the President and the Council of Ministers.

### C. Protection of ministerial advice

Under **Article 74(2)**:

- Courts cannot inquire into whether ministerial advice was given to the President or what that advice was.
- This does **not** make all resulting executive action immune from judicial review.

---

## 7. Relationship with Parliament

The Prime Minister:

- Leads the government’s legislative and policy agenda.
- Explains and defends government policies in Parliament.
- Advises the President, through the ministerial system, concerning summoning and prorogation.
- May recommend dissolution of the Lok Sabha.

### Formal powers versus political advice

| Action | Constitutional position |
|---|---|
| Summoning Parliament | Formally done by the President |
| Proroguing a House | Formally done by the President |
| Dissolving the Lok Sabha | Formally done by the President |
| Recommending Lok Sabha dissolution | A role of the Prime Minister |

The **Rajya Sabha is not subject to dissolution**.

### Leader of the House in the Lok Sabha

- If the Prime Minister belongs to the Lok Sabha, the Prime Minister functions as its **Leader of the House**.
- If the Prime Minister does not belong to the Lok Sabha, a minister who is a Lok Sabha member is nominated by the Prime Minister to perform that role.

> A Prime Minister belonging to the Rajya Sabha is **not automatically the Leader of the House in the Lok Sabha**.

---

## 8. Collective Responsibility

Under **Article 75(3)**:

> The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.

This means:

- The ministry must retain the confidence of the Lok Sabha.
- Ministers must publicly support collective government decisions or leave the ministry.
- A successful no-confidence motion in the Lok Sabha requires the ministry to resign.

### Important distinctions

- Collective responsibility is to the **Lok Sabha**, not to both Houses jointly.
- Collective responsibility does not shift to the Rajya Sabha when the Prime Minister belongs to that House.
- The Rajya Sabha can scrutinise and criticise the government, but cannot remove it through a no-confidence motion.
- Collective responsibility applies to **both coalition and single-party governments**.

---

## 9. Other Important Roles

The Prime Minister:

- Provides overall political leadership to the Union government.
- Plays a major role in shaping foreign policy.
- Acts as a principal spokesperson of the government.
- Coordinates responses to major national problems and crises.
- Plays an important role in Centre–State coordination.
- Leads the governing party or coalition in the parliamentary sphere.

> The Prime Minister need not also hold the organisational office of **president of the ruling political party**.

---

## 10. Prime Minister and National Emergency

Under **Article 352(3)**:

- The President cannot proclaim a National Emergency unless the **Union Cabinet’s decision has been communicated in writing**.
- For this purpose, the Cabinet consists of the **Prime Minister and other ministers of Cabinet rank** appointed under Article 75.
- The Prime Minister’s individual advice cannot replace the required written Cabinet decision.

> **Cabinet decision in writing**, not merely the Prime Minister’s personal recommendation, is the constitutional requirement.

---

## 11. Selected Historical Facts

### Acting Prime Minister

**Gulzari Lal Nanda** served as acting Prime Minister twice:

- **1964:** After Jawaharlal Nehru’s death.
- **1966:** After Lal Bahadur Shastri’s death.

After Indira Gandhi’s assassination in **1984**, **Rajiv Gandhi** was appointed Prime Minister.

## 12. Quick Revision: Common Prelims Traps

| Statement | Correct position |
|---|---|
| The Prime Minister must belong to the Lok Sabha | **Incorrect:** May belong to either House |
| A non-MP cannot be appointed Prime Minister | **Incorrect:** An otherwise eligible non-MP can be appointed, subject to the six-month rule |
| The Prime Minister has a fixed five-year term | **Incorrect:** No fixed constitutional tenure |
| The Prime Minister is directly elected by the people | **Incorrect:** Appointed by the President |
| The largest party’s leader must always be appointed | **Incorrect:** Ability to command Lok Sabha confidence is central |
| The senior-most minister automatically succeeds a deceased Prime Minister | **Incorrect:** No automatic constitutional succession |
| The ministry is responsible to the Prime Minister’s House | **Incorrect:** Collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha |
| The President can repeatedly reject reconsidered ministerial advice | **Incorrect:** Advice after reconsideration is binding |
| The Prime Minister formally dissolves the Lok Sabha | **Incorrect:** The President formally dissolves it |
| A minister may vote in either House | **Incorrect:** Ministerial office alone does not confer voting rights |
| The Prime Minister is excluded from the 15% ministerial limit | **Incorrect:** The Prime Minister is included |
| National Emergency requires only the Prime Minister’s advice | **Incorrect:** A written Union Cabinet decision is required |

<strong>B.</strong> The Prime Minister advises on ministerial appointments, coordinates the ministry and, by resigning, brings about the resignation of the ministry as a whole<br>
<strong>C.</strong> The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Prime Minister instead of the Lok Sabha<br>
<strong>D.</strong> The Prime Minister personally exercises every constitutional power assigned to the President
</div>

<br>

**Answer: B**

> **Explanation:** The Prime Minister’s leadership rests on the central role in selecting ministers, distributing portfolios and coordinating policy. The ministry’s existence is tied to the Prime Minister, but its collective responsibility remains to the Lok Sabha.

### Q25. Consider the following statements:

2. The President appoints the successor Prime Minister.
3. The Constitution requires a nationwide direct election to fill a vacancy in the office of Prime Minister.
4. If the ruling party commands a Lok Sabha majority and chooses a new leader, parliamentary convention guides the President to appoint that leader.

Which of the statements given above are incorrect?

<div>
<strong>A.</strong> 1 and 2 only<br>
<strong>B.</strong> 2 and 4 only<br>
<strong>C.</strong> 3 and 4 only<br>

### Q29. Assertion (A): A member of the Rajya Sabha can serve as Prime Minister of India.

<div>
<strong>A.</strong> Both A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A.<br>
<strong>B.</strong> Both A and R are correct, but R is not the correct explanation of A.<br>

<strong>D.</strong> A is incorrect, but R is correct.
</div>

**Answer: B**

> **Explanation:** Both statements are correct. A Rajya Sabha member can be Prime Minister because the constitutional framework permits ministerial membership of either House. ### Q30. Assertion (A): A Prime Minister who retains the confidence of the Lok Sabha must nevertheless resign if the Rajya Sabha passes a motion expressing no confidence in the government.

<strong>C.</strong> A is correct, but R is incorrect.<br>
<strong>D.</strong> A is incorrect, but R is correct.
</div>

<br>

**Answer: D**

> **Explanation:** Collective responsibility operates in relation to the Lok Sabha. An adverse expression of opinion in the Rajya Sabha does not, by itself, constitutionally require the ministry to resign. Thus, the Assertion is incorrect and the Reason is correct.

### Q32. Assertion (A): An otherwise eligible person who is not a member of either House of Parliament may be appointed Prime Minister.
### Reason (R): Article 75(5) permits such a person to remain a minister without entering either House for twelve consecutive months.

<div>
<strong>A.</strong> Both A and R are correct, and R is the correct explanation of A.<br>
<strong>B.</strong> Both A and R are correct, but R is not the correct explanation of A.<br>
<strong>C.</strong> A is correct, but R is incorrect.<br>
<strong>D.</strong> A is incorrect, but R is correct.
</div>

<br>

**Answer: C**

> **Explanation:** Appointment without existing parliamentary membership is permissible for an otherwise eligible person. However, Article 75(5) specifies six consecutive months, not twelve. The person must become a member of either House within that period to continue as a minister.
